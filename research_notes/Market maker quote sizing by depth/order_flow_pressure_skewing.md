# Measuring buy/sell pressure (OFI, trade imbalance, own-fill imbalance) and using it to skew quote prices and sizes

Scope note: these notes cover (1) predictive power of order-flow / book-imbalance measures with the actual regression forms and numbers, (2) estimating toxicity from a maker's own fills (VPIN, markouts, fill-conditional imbalance), (3) explicit rules from the literature for turning a flow signal into price skews and size asymmetry, (4) online / Bayesian / windowed estimation from sparse own-fill data. Every number below is taken from the cited primary text (arXiv HTML/ar5iv renderings, or the author PDFs extracted locally). Where a source could only be read partially, that is flagged in Gaps.

## KQ1. How predictive is order-flow imbalance (OFI) / trade imbalance for short-horizon price moves, and what is the functional form?

### Takeaway
Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov's OFI explains ~65% of 10-second mid-price variance in a *linear* regression (ΔP ≈ β·OFI, β ≈ c/depth with c≈0.45–0.5), about double what trade imbalance alone explains (32%); queue imbalance at the touch predicts the direction of the next mid move with AUC ≈0.75–0.80 for large-tick stocks (P(up|I≈+1)≈0.8–0.9) but only ≈0.58–0.64 for small-tick stocks; deeper levels add out-of-sample value, especially for large-tick names, when regularized.

### Cited Findings
- **OFI definition (Cont, Kukanov, Stoikov 2014, arXiv:1011.6402, J. Financial Econometrics).** Each best-quote update n contributes e_n = 1{P^b_n ≥ P^b_{n-1}} q^b_n − 1{P^b_n ≤ P^b_{n-1}} q^b_{n-1} − 1{P^a_n ≤ P^a_{n-1}} q^a_n + 1{P^a_n ≥ P^a_{n-1}} q^a_{n-1}; OFI_k = Σ e_n over events in window (t_{k-1}, t_k]. A market sell and a bid cancellation of the same size are treated identically. Mid-price change is measured in ticks, ΔP_k = (P_k − P_{k-1})/δ. — [ar5iv 1011.6402](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.6402)
- **Stylized model and linear form.** In a book with constant depth D beyond the best quote, ΔP = OFI/(2D) + ε, i.e. β = 1/(2D). Empirical model: ΔP_k = β·OFI_k + ε_k estimated by OLS on 273 half-hour subsamples per stock (~180 obs each). — [ar5iv 1011.6402](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.6402)
- **Depth relation.** Fitting β_i = c/AD_i^λ + ν_i (AD = average of bid and ask best-queue sizes over the interval, divided by 2): grand-mean λ̂ = 0.98 (λ=1 not rejected for 35/50 stocks), grand-mean ĉ = 0.45 vs stylized 0.5; R² of this cross-interval fit 74% on average (poor for APOL, AZO, CME: 2%, 13%, 2%). — [ar5iv 1011.6402](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.6402)
- **Data and R².** 50 randomly chosen S&P 500 stocks, NYSE TAQ, April 2010 (21 days), 10-second windows. Average R² = 65%; per-stock range ≈44% (WIN) to 79% (BAC); e.g. SLB 76%, AZO 47%, CME 35%. β passes a 5% z-test in 97% of subsamples. Excluding price-changing events (to rule out tautology) R² falls to 35–60%. — [ar5iv 1011.6402](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.6402)
- **Linearity.** Adding a quadratic term OFI·|OFI| raises average R² from 65% to 68% ("barely noticeable"); the quadratic coefficient is significant in only 9% of samples. Authors state they found no evidence of non-linear price impact for OFI or TI at the 10-s window. (Quadratic terms do show up in transaction-price regressions, attributed partly to sampling at trade times.) — [ar5iv 1011.6402](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.6402)
- **OFI vs trade imbalance (TI).** Average R²: OFI 65% vs TI 32%; both together 67%. In the joint regression the average t-stat of TI drops from 4.18 to 1.16 and TI is significant in only 31% of subsamples. Traded volume alone: R² 23%; |OFI| alone explains 58% of |ΔP|. — [ar5iv 1011.6402](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.6402)
- **Horizon.** Tested Δt from 10 quote updates (<0.5 s) to 10 minutes; fit "generally increases" with Δt (no table). In trade-count aggregation on transaction prices, OFI R² rises 14% (L=2 trades) → 38% (L=5) → 51% (L=10) while TI gives 1%, 8%, 13%. — [ar5iv 1011.6402](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.6402)
- **Multi-level OFI (Xu, Gould, Howison 2019, arXiv:1907.06230).** MLOFI^m = Σ_events [ΔW^m − ΔV^m] computed level-by-level (M=1 reduces to CKS OFI). Regression ΔP = α + Σ_{m=1..10} β^m MLOFI^m + ε, OLS and Ridge (λ by 5-fold CV; 1.3 for CSCO to 139 for AMZN). Data: LOBSTER/Nasdaq, AMZN, TSLA, NFLX (small-tick), ORCL, CSCO, MU (large-tick), 4 Jan–30 Dec 2016, 10:00–15:30, 10-s intervals inside 30-min windows (2,772 regressions per stock). — [ar5iv 1907.06230](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.06230)
- **MLOFI results.** In-sample adjusted R² at M=10: AMZN/TSLA ≈0.65 (OLS)/0.60 (Ridge), NFLX ≈0.90/0.85, large-tick stocks ≈1. Out-of-sample RMSE (ticks) OFI → MLOFI-Ridge: AMZN 9.72→8.05 (−17%), TSLA 5.35→4.53 (−15%), NFLX 2.03→1.41 (−31%), ORCL 0.25→0.08 (−68%), CSCO 0.19→0.05 (−74%), MU 0.22→0.08 (−64%). Ridge coefficients: AMZN β¹…β¹⁰ 2.17→1.01 (decreasing), TSLA 1.28→0.60, NFLX flat ≈0.46, ORCL 0.05→0.09, CSCO 0.03→0.06, MU 0.04→0.07 (slightly increasing with depth). Inter-level correlations >0.5 (small tick) and >0.7 (large tick); OLS overfits beyond M≈5 for AMZN/TSLA. Conclusion: for large-tick stocks all ten levels contribute; for small-tick, the level nearest the spread matters most but deeper levels still help. — [ar5iv 1907.06230](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.06230)
- **Queue imbalance → next mid move (Gould & Bonart 2016, arXiv:1512.03492).** I = (n^b − n^a)/(n^b + n^a) at the touch, sampled at a uniformly random time before each mid change. Logistic fit P(up|I) = 1/(1+exp(−(x0 + x1·I))). x1 (s.e.): MSFT 2.49 (0.04), INTC 2.56, MU 2.03, CSCO 2.73, ORCL 2.25 (large-tick); GOOG 0.54, AMZN 0.85, TSLA 0.60, PCLN 0.50, NFLX 0.65 (small-tick); x0 ≈ 0 (±0.06). Data: LOBSTER/Nasdaq, all of 2014, 10:00–15:30, 25,200 samples per stock, 80/20 split. — [ar5iv 1512.03492](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1512.03492)
- **Magnitude by tick size.** Large-tick: P(up) ≈ 0.8–0.9 near I=+1, out-of-sample AUC 0.752–0.805 (MSFT 0.762, INTC 0.798, MU 0.752, CSCO 0.805, ORCL 0.770), mean squared residual ≈0.18–0.20 vs 0.25 null (20–30% reduction). Small-tick: P(up) ≈ 0.6–0.7 near I=+1, AUC 0.581–0.642, residual reduction only 2–6%. Explanation: with spread ≈ 1 tick, no in-spread order arrivals, so price changes come from queue depletion which I captures; queues are also longer. — [ar5iv 1512.03492](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1512.03492)
- **Shape.** Local logistic fits for large-tick stocks are steep for I in roughly [−0.25, 0.25] and flatter outside (two regimes); small-tick curves are non-monotone for all but PCLN. — [ar5iv 1512.03492](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1512.03492)
- **Imbalance → trade direction and post-trade price change (Cartea, Donnelly, Jaimungal 2018, Applied Math. Finance 25(1):1–35).** ρ_t = (V^b_t − V^a_t)/(V^b_t + V^a_t) using best-level volumes; regimes sell-heavy ρ∈[−1,−1/3), neutral [−1/3,1/3], buy-heavy (1/3,1]. Table B.9 average MO intensities (Jan 2014, Nasdaq, first/last 30 min removed), columns = buy intensity in (sell-heavy, neutral, buy-heavy) then sell intensity in the same regimes: INTC buys 0.024 / 0.048 / 0.232, sells 0.288 / 0.056 / 0.021; ORCL buys 0.022 / 0.041 / 0.181, sells 0.213 / 0.037 / 0.015; NTAP buys 0.031/0.050/0.167, sells 0.133/0.045/0.031; AAPL (small-tick) buys 0.223/0.257/0.276, sells 0.293/0.271/0.228; GOOG buys 0.060/0.090/0.112, sells 0.102/0.095/0.071. So for large-tick names buy-MO intensity is ~10× higher in buy-heavy than sell-heavy books; for small-tick AAPL the effect is ~20%. — [ORA author copy](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:006addde-3a03-4d75-89c1-04b59026e1c0/files/me4008e0ecca779b45d59231ebca3e69c); journal record [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apmtfi/v25y2018i1p1-35.html)
- **Post-MO mid change.** Mid-price change 10 ms after an MO has a positive (negative) bias in buy-heavy (sell-heavy) regimes, and this persists when conditioning only on buy MOs (Fig. 4 c,d), i.e. imbalance predicts both the sign of the next MO and the mid move after it. Between 91.6% (GOOG) and 99.9% of MOs are filled entirely at the best price, so LOs deeper than the touch have <0.1%–8.4% chance of execution per MO. — [ORA author copy](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:006addde-3a03-4d75-89c1-04b59026e1c0/files/me4008e0ecca779b45d59231ebca3e69c)
- **Imbalance vs. waiting time and size of move (Lipton, Pesavento, Sotiropoulos 2013, arXiv:1312.0514; VOD.L, Q1 2012).** Average mid change until the next move is roughly linear in I and well below the spread, reaching about a third of the spread in a highly imbalanced book; highly imbalanced books have shorter waits to the next mid change; probability of an unfavourable move for a near-side resting order rises to "almost 90%" at high imbalance. Calibrated parameters ρ_xz = −ρ_yz = 0.8, ρ_xy = −0.1, φ₀ = 3.5 s^{1/2}; queue depletion interacting with trade flow accounts for ≈60% of the theoretical spread lost by passive fills. — [ar5iv 1312.0514](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1312.0514)
- **Imbalance before trades, AstraZeneca (Lehalle & Mounjid 2017, arXiv:1610.00261).** NASDAQ OMX Stockholm AZN, Jan–Sep 2013, 376,672 trades with labelled participants: highly linear positive relation between imbalance just before a trade and the mid move over the next 50 trades, but "too weak for statistical arbitrage" once spread and fees are paid. — [ar5iv 1610.00261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00261)
- **Deep OFI (Kolm, Turiel, Westray 2023, Math. Finance 33(4):1044–1081, DOI 10.1111/mafi.12413).** 115 Nasdaq stocks; models trained on order-flow (OFI-type) stationary features significantly outperform most models trained on raw order-book states; effective forecast horizon of stock-specific forecasts ≈ two average price changes; more "information-rich" microstructures are easier to predict. — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/mathfi/v33y2023i4p1044-1081.html)
- **Fill probability vs. queue sizes, BTC perp (arXiv:2502.18625).** Live Binance BTCUSDT-perp data (Feb 2024, 232,897 min-size touch orders, 127,051 filled): fill probability ranges from ≈30% (large near-side queue, small far-side queue) to >90% (small near-side, large far-side); an OLS fit on normalized queue sizes and imbalance has R² = 0.946. — [arXiv 2502.18625](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625)

### Inferences
- A practical pressure signal for a maker is CKS OFI aggregated over a short window, scaled by current depth: expected mid move in ticks ≈ 0.45·OFI/AD (AD = average best-queue size/2). This gives a direct "fair-value shift" in ticks without any fitted model, and it is linear, so EWMA accumulation of e_n is a reasonable online implementation.
- Queue imbalance at the touch is the single most powerful feature for large-tick instruments (AUC ~0.8); for small-tick instruments one should rely more on OFI over a window and on deeper-level MLOFI, since the touch imbalance alone barely beats 0.5.
- Trade imbalance alone is a weak predictor (R² 32%) because it ignores cancellations and additions; a maker's fill stream is a trade-flow signal and will inherit this weakness unless combined with book-flow OFI.

### Gaps
- CKS do not tabulate R² by horizon; only the qualitative statement "increases with Δt".
- Kolm et al. R² / accuracy numbers per horizon were not accessible (paywalled; only the abstract was read).
- The Gould–Bonart P(up|I≈−1) values are inferred by symmetry (x0≈0), not stated.
- Cartea–Donnelly–Jaimungal give the post-MO price change only as histograms (Fig. 4); no numeric mean is printed in the text.

## KQ2. Microprice: adjusting fair value from book imbalance (Stoikov 2018 and follow-ons)

### Takeaway
Stoikov's micro-price is the limit of expected future mid-prices conditioned on (imbalance I, spread S): P_micro = M + Σ_k g_k(I,S), computed by an absorbing Markov chain from estimated transition matrices; it is a martingale, horizon-independent, lives between bid and ask, and needs less data than direct averaging of future mid changes.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition.** P_micro_t = lim_{i→∞} P^i_t with P^i_t = E[M_{τ_i} | F_t], where τ_1, τ_2, … are the (random) times the mid changes and F_t = (M_t, I_t, S_t), I_t = Q^b/(Q^b+Q^a), S_t = (P^a − P^b)/2. Assumption: dynamics of (M, I, S) independent of level M. Main theorem: P^i_t = M_t + Σ_{k=1}^{i} g_k(I_t,S_t), g_1(I,S) = E[M_{τ1} − M_t | I,S], g_{i+1}(I,S) = E[g_i(I_{τ1},S_{τ1}) | I,S]. — [Stoikov slides (Gatheral60)](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~ajacquie/Gatheral60/Slides/Gatheral60%20-%20Stoikov.pdf); journal: Quantitative Finance 18(12):1959–1966, 2018, DOI 10.1080/14697688.2018.1489139 ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v18y2018i12p1959-1966.html))
- **Toy results.** If mid moves are independent of I: P_micro = M. If I is a Brownian motion on [0,1] absorbed at 0/1 with the mid jumping ±1 w.p. ½ on absorption and S=1: P_micro = M + I − 1/2 (i.e. a linear tilt of up to half a spread). — [Stoikov slides](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~ajacquie/Gatheral60/Slides/Gatheral60%20-%20Stoikov.pdf)
- **Discrete Markov computation.** Discretize I into n bins and S into m values; estimate Q (transient transitions with no mid change), R1 (absorbing: mid change k), R2 (mid change with new imbalance). Then g_1 = (1−Q)^{-1} R1 k, B = (1−Q)^{-1} R2, g_{i+1} = B g_i; convergence requires B's stationary distribution W satisfies W g_1 = 0, enforced by symmetrizing g_1(i_I,i_S) = 1 − g_1(n−i_I, i_S). Spectral form: P_micro − M = Σ_{i≥2} exp(λ_i) B_i g_1. — [Stoikov slides](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~ajacquie/Gatheral60/Slides/Gatheral60%20-%20Stoikov.pdf)
- **Data / validation.** BAC (large-tick) and CVX (small-tick), March 2011 quotes; G* estimated on the first day of March and compared out-of-sample to averages of M_{t+60s}−M_t, M_{t+300s}−M_t, M_{t+600s}−M_t grouped by (I,S) over the month. Stated conclusions: micro-price is a good predictor of future mid, fits very different microstructures, "needs less data to converge than averaging mid price changes over fixed horizons", is horizon independent, and "seems to live between the bid and the ask". — [Stoikov slides](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~ajacquie/Gatheral60/Slides/Gatheral60%20-%20Stoikov.pdf)
- **Why not the weighted mid.** M_w = I·P^a + (1−I)·P^b (Gatheral–Oomen 2009) is not a martingale, is noisy, and has counter-intuitive examples (a cancel on the ask moves the "fair" price down). — [Stoikov slides](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~ajacquie/Gatheral60/Slides/Gatheral60%20-%20Stoikov.pdf)
- **Secondary restatement with recent data (arXiv:2411.13594).** Restates P_micro = M + g(I,S) via G1 = (1−Q)^{-1} R K, B = (1−Q)^{-1} T, G* = G1 + Σ B^i G1, symmetrization by mirrored observations; TSLA/TEM Databento L3, Sept 2024; an error-correcting extension improves L2 error vs N-steps-ahead mid by roughly 10–20% (table rows imply 5–37%). The paper gives no numeric g(I,S) values in ticks for Stoikov's estimator. — [arXiv 2411.13594](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13594v1)
- **Related: Lehalle–Mounjid define a microprice as the expected future price given imbalance, P_{+∞}, with an adverse-selection parameter α scaling how much it depends on imbalance (α=4 in simulations).** — [ar5iv 1610.00261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00261)

### Inferences
- For a maker, the micro-price adjustment g(I,S) is the natural "fair value skew" input: quote around P_micro rather than M. Its magnitude is bounded by about half a spread in the toy model and empirically "between bid and ask", consistent with Lipton et al.'s ~⅓-spread mean move at extreme imbalance.
- Because the Markov estimator only needs transition counts over (I,S) bins, it is well suited to online updating with counts (the slides explicitly claim lower data requirements than horizon averaging).

### Gaps
- The journal/SSRN text of Stoikov (2018) was not accessible (SSRN 403), so numeric G*(I,S) values for BAC/CVX are not reported here; only the figure descriptions on the slides.

## KQ3. How should placement and size respond to imbalance (Lehalle–Mounjid and related)?

### Takeaway
Lehalle–Mounjid's optimal policy for a resting buy order is to cancel and wait when imbalance is strongly against it (adverse selection) and to stay when it is favourable, with faster traders gaining more; Cartea–Donnelly–Jaimungal's control gives explicit posting regions (post the sell in sell-heavy books, withdraw the sell in buy-heavy books unless inventory is high); Pulido–Rosenbaum–Sfendourakis show the optimal *volumes* at the touch are monotone in the efficient-price offset, i.e. the maker should herself quote a predictive imbalance.

### Cited Findings
- **Lehalle & Mounjid setup (arXiv:1610.00261).** One small buy LO at the best bid of a large-tick stock (spread = 1 tick), forced market-order execution at horizon T_f; state = queue sizes; Imb = (Q^Bid − Q^Ask)/(Q^Bid + Q^Ask); four counting processes (insert/cancel on same and opposite side) with imbalance-dependent intensities; controls each period: continue (c) or stop/cancel-and-wait (s); objective = expected gain vs the microprice P_{+∞} (adverse-selection penalty α); solved by dynamic programming on a Markov chain. — [ar5iv 1610.00261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00261)
- **Policy result.** When imbalance is strongly negative (pressure down, for a buy), optimal policy cancels to avoid buying right before a drop and waits to reinsert; improvement over "never cancel" is ≈0 when imbalance is strongly positive and positive when strongly negative; optimal strategies last longer (cancelling postpones execution); more remaining time helps with diminishing returns; near T the policy becomes more active because crossing the spread becomes costly. — [ar5iv 1610.00261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00261)
- **Simulation parameters.** Horizon 20 periods, q=1, α=4; constant-intensity case λ⁺=0.06, λ⁻=0.5; imbalance-dependent case λ0⁺=0.06, λ0⁻=0.5, β⁺=0.075, β⁻=0.25. — [ar5iv 1610.00261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00261)
- **Latency.** Cost of latency = optimal value minus value when controls can only be updated every τ periods; it rises with τ and with α; "speed can be seen as a protection to adverse selection"; the faster of two agents acting on the same imbalance criterion earns more. — [ar5iv 1610.00261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00261)
- **Size.** The paper treats a single small order (q=1) and gives no size rule; it is placement/timing only. — [ar5iv 1610.00261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00261)
- **Cartea–Donnelly–Jaimungal posting rule.** Controls γ^±(t,q,J) ∈ {0,1} (post/not post at the touch) with feedback form: post a sell iff Δ/2 − ε⁺(J) + Φ⁺₁(t,q,J) > Φ⁺₀(t,q,J) (and q ≠ −Q̄), symmetric for buys, where Δ = spread, ε^±(J) = expected mid change right after a buy/sell MO in imbalance-spread state J, and Φ terms are inventory-penalty corrections. Rule of thumb: in a sell-heavy regime post the sell when Δ/2 − ε⁺(J) > 0 (half-spread exceeds expected post-MO price rise); in a buy-heavy regime it is "usually not optimal to post a sell order" because the expected mid increase exceeds the half-spread, so the agent withdraws that side ("no-trade regions" in both heavy regimes, Fig. 6) and lets inventory appreciate; sell region is larger when the spread is 2 ticks than 1 tick. — [ORA author copy](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:006addde-3a03-4d75-89c1-04b59026e1c0/files/me4008e0ecca779b45d59231ebca3e69c)
- **CDJ performance.** Zero-intelligence two-sided posting at the touch has negative expected PnL for INTC/ORCL (Jul–Dec 2014) across all inventory limits. With imbalance (n_Z = 3 or 5 states, spread 1–2 ticks, Q̄ = 50), annualized Sharpe for INTC rises from −7.5…−17 (n_Z=1, Table 3) to 1.9…25.1 (n_Z=3, Table 4) and 4.5…24.5 (n_Z=5, Table 5) as the running inventory penalty φ grows from 0 to 1e−5; ORCL 0.9…26 → 11.7…34. With a pessimistic back-of-queue fill model (Table 6 fill probabilities, e.g. EBAY p=0.28 neutral, 0.40/0.20 in a buy-heavy state for sell/buy LOs; INTC 0.05–0.32) Sharpe falls to ≈−2…2.4 (INTC) and 3.5…12.2 (ORCL), still above the zero-intelligence benchmark. n_Z=3 and n_Z=5 perform about the same. — [ORA author copy](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:006addde-3a03-4d75-89c1-04b59026e1c0/files/me4008e0ecca779b45d59231ebca3e69c)
- **Optimal quoted volumes (Pulido, Rosenbaum, Sfendourakis, arXiv:2307.15599; Finance & Stochastics 2026, DOI 10.1007/s00780-026-00585-9).** Single maker on a large-tick stock controls only the volumes q^b, q^a at the touch; efficient price S (Bachelier), Y = S − P the signed distance of efficient price from mid; MO intensities depend on Y; CARA utility with inventory bound. Results: optimal ask volume is nonincreasing in y and optimal bid volume nondecreasing in y; the maker may stop quoting a side (selling when y > δ/2 loses money); with negative inventory she buys more and sells less and vice versa; the quoted imbalance I = (q^b − q^a)/(q^b + q^a) rises with y (confirmed numerically; roughly affine in some cases when bounds are ignored); higher γ or σ shrink quote sizes; higher MO intensity allows larger quotes, even at expected loss per trade when an offsetting trade is likely. — [ar5iv 2307.15599](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.15599)
- **Queue-reactive intensities (Huang, Lehalle, Rosenbaum 2015, arXiv:1312.0563).** Euronext Paris, France Telecom / Alcatel-Lucent, Jan 2010–Mar 2012: market-order intensity at the best queue decreases roughly exponentially with that queue's size; cancellation intensity increases and is concave up to ≈25 AES; market orders are higher when the opposite side is abundant; insertions are higher when the opposite queue is empty. The paper does not estimate fill probability as a function of imbalance directly. — [ar5iv 1312.0563](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1312.0563)
- **Broker placement rule (Lipton et al.).** Suggests keeping a passive order at moderate imbalance but crossing the spread when the book is highly imbalanced against the order. — [ar5iv 1312.0514](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1312.0514)

### Inferences
- The consistent prescription across the control papers: the side the flow is about to hit (ask in a buy-heavy book) should be *reduced or pulled* unless the half-spread exceeds the expected post-trade move (Δ/2 > ε⁺), and the side that flow is moving away from should be *kept or enlarged* because it earns the spread with little adverse selection. Pulido et al. formalize this as monotone volume functions of the fair-value offset y, i.e. size_ask ↓ and size_bid ↑ as fair value rises above mid.
- Lehalle–Mounjid's result that fast cancel/reinsert cycles are the main defence implies that a slower maker (who cannot react within the imbalance's predictive window) should substitute *standing* size asymmetry for cancellation: post less on the side that will be adversely selected, since it cannot cancel in time.

### Gaps
- Lehalle–Mounjid report no explicit fill probabilities or a size rule; the AZN "price profile" numbers are only in figures.
- Pulido et al. give no closed form for optimal volumes; only monotonicity results and numerical plots (not reproduced in the HTML read).

## KQ4. Estimating toxicity / informed flow from the maker's own fills (VPIN, markouts, fill-imbalance)

### Takeaway
VPIN is a rolling volume-bucket imbalance (|V_buy − V_sell| averaged over 50 buckets of 1/50 daily volume, trades signed by bulk-volume classification) that correlates ≈0.40 with next-bucket absolute returns on E-mini S&P; markouts (mid at t+Δ minus fill price) directly measure adverse selection and are typically negative at the touch (≈ −0.45 ticks on 10-yr Treasury futures; ≈ −0.8 bp at 1 s on BTC-perp); the imbalance a participant accepts at the moment of its fills reveals how selective (or urgent) it is.

### Cited Findings
- **VPIN construction (Easley, López de Prado, O'Hara 2012, RFS 25(5):1457–1493, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhs053).** Trades aggregated into volume buckets; buy share within each time bar from the bulk volume classification V^B = V·Z((ΔP)/σ_ΔP) with Z the standard-normal CDF and σ_ΔP the s.d. of bar-to-bar price changes (volume split equally if no price change); VPIN = Σ_{τ=1..n}|V^S_τ − V^B_τ| / (n·V). Chosen parameters: bucket size V = 1/50 of average daily volume, rolling window n = 50 buckets (≈ one day of volume); 1-minute time bars; robust to bar length from 1 to 240 minutes. — [author PDF, NYU Stern](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/con_035928.pdf)
- **VPIN statistics.** E-mini S&P 500 (CME) and WTI (NYMEX), 1 Jan 2008–6 Jun 2011: mean VPIN 0.2251 (S&P) / 0.2191 (WTI), s.d. 0.0576 / 0.0455, AR(1) 0.9958 / 0.9932, CDF quantiles for S&P: 0.1→0.158, 0.5→0.218, 0.9→0.302. VPIN CDFs before vs after the flash crash nearly identical; on 6 May 2010 VPIN reached ≈0.5 (CDF ≈0.99). — [author PDF](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/con_035928.pdf)
- **Predictive power.** Pearson correlation between ln VPIN and the absolute return over the following bucket = 0.400 on 44,537 observations for the (50 buckets/day, 250-bucket sample) combination; correlation rises with sample length and, less so, with buckets per day. When VPIN is in its bottom quartile, next-bucket |return| is in 0–0.25% more than 90% of the time; the upper VPIN quartile contains >84% of all |returns| > 0.75%. With (50,250), 51.84% of entries into an upper-quartile 5%-tile see an intermediate |return| > 0.75% before VPIN leaves the tile; with (10 buckets/day, 350) it is 78.57%. — [author PDF](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/con_035928.pdf)
- **Stated use for makers.** "Market makers … can use the VPIN metric as a real-time risk management tool"; VPIN rose in the hours before the 6 May 2010 flash crash and contributed to liquidity-provider withdrawal; in the PIN model the spread is driven by the probability an order is informed (PIN = αμ/(αμ + 2ε)). — [author PDF](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/con_035928.pdf)
- **Critique.** Andersen & Bondarenko (CREATES WP 2011-50 / 2013-43; J. Financial Markets 2014) argue VPIN is a weak predictor of short-run volatility, that it peaked *after* rather than before the flash crash, and that its predictive content mostly reflects a mechanical link to trading intensity; the search summary also notes they distinguish tick-rule VPIN from "BV-VPIN". — [CREATES rp11_50](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/11/rp11_50.pdf), [CREATES rp13_43](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/13/rp13_43.pdf) (only search-level summaries were readable; see Gaps)
- **Markout definition (arXiv:2608.18195, multi-level RL market making).** For a limit sell filled at p^f_t: markout = p^f_t − p_{t+Δt}; for a buy: p_{t+Δt} − p^f_t (mid), Δt = 30 s; positive favours the maker; "markouts measure the extent of adverse selection for limit order fills." In a 1-tick spread with stable prices the expected markout of a touch fill is ≈0.5 tick. Simulated expected markouts (ticks) for a touch-only agent fall from 0.33 (noise traders, 2 lots) to 0.23 and −0.06 (noise + tactical + strategic traders, 2 and 20 lots); second-level fills have better markouts (0.59–0.90) but are rarer. — [arXiv 2608.18195](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18195)
- **Negative drift of fills (DeLise 2024, arXiv:2407.16527).** Model: mid moves ±1 tick or 0; a resting buy at the best bid fills with certainty on a down move but only with Bernoulli rate R_f < 1 otherwise, so E[drift | fill] = (R_f·P(U) − P(D))/P(fill) < 0. 10-yr Treasury futures, 1-s bins: P(U)=P(D)=0.0173, P(flat)=0.965, R_f=0.018, P(fill|down)=0.99 → theory −0.48 ticks, empirical −0.45 ticks post-fill drift; live TT simulation 21 Nov 2023: mean mid change over 100 events after a fill ≈ −0.0065 price units (≈ −0.42 of a 1/64 tick) vs ≈0 at random times; ~⅓ of orders never fill. — [arXiv 2407.16527](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16527v1)
- **Fill probability vs. post-fill return (arXiv:2502.18625).** Binance BTC-perp live data: markout = return from limit price to mid 1 s after fill, ex-rebate. Front-of-queue fills in a large near-side queue with small far-side queue average −0.06 bp; back-of-queue fills in the same state −0.78 bp; average over all orders ≈ −0.8 bp (−0.3 bp after rebate); orders with negative 5-s returns fill far more often. Strategy returns per trade net of fees: naive maker −0.43 bp, imbalance maker w/o cancel −0.47 bp, with cancel −0.49 bp, imbalance taker −1.96 bp; the naive maker lost ~60% in 3.3 days. A "reversal" classifier (logistic; label = fills whose first post-fill mid move is favourable; features: multi-horizon returns 100 ms–300 s, volatility, trade-volume patterns) is proposed to pick the good fills. Cancellation rules only help against slow adverse imbalance drift, not sudden large takes. — [arXiv 2502.18625](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625)
- **Imbalance accepted at own fills as a selectivity metric (Lehalle & Mounjid).** Average neutralized imbalance at limit-order fills on AZN: institutional brokers sells ≈0.57 / buys ≈−0.52; global banks 0.35 / −0.38; high-frequency participants 0.32 / −0.33 (about half of what brokers accept); within HFPs, market makers accept more adverse imbalance than proprietary traders, who suffer least adverse selection; HFP buy fills are preceded by rising prices (consistent with re-insertion just before execution), broker buy fills by falling prices. Makers accept adverse imbalance because spread gains offset adverse selection (citing Menkveld 2013). — [ar5iv 1610.00261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.00261)
- **Bayesian informed-fraction from fills (arXiv:2607.11888).** Each fill i yields Z_i = |S_{τ_i+h} − S_{τ_i}| modelled as a mixture π·F_info + (1−π)·F_noise (F_noise = |N(0,σ²h)|, F_info folded normal with mean α_info); prior π ~ Beta(a0,b0); posterior mean π̂_n = (a0 + Σ 1{Z_i > θ_c})/(a0 + b0 + n) with θ_c the midpoint of the two expected displacements; adverse-selection cost α̂_n = π̂_n·α_info + (1−π̂_n)·c_ℓ σ√Δt enters the optimal half-spread; MSE ≤ π(1−π)/n + O(1/n²), 95% credible width ≈ 3.92√(π(1−π)/n), cumulative regret O(√N). — [arXiv 2607.11888](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11888)
- **Simulation-based classification.** Lalor & Swishchuk classify a fill as adverse when the next change in marked-to-market value is negative, and argue independent simulation of prices and orders overstates short-term performance. — [arXiv 2409.12721 (search summary)](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12721v1)

### Inferences
- The simplest own-fill toxicity estimators that the literature supports are (a) a rolling signed-fill imbalance (VPIN applied to one's own executed volume or to tape volume), and (b) short-horizon markouts per fill, which can be averaged by side and by book state (e.g. near/far queue bins as in 2502.18625). Both are first-order statistics that need no model fitting.
- The Lehalle–Mounjid "imbalance at fill" statistic is a cheap diagnostic: if the average book imbalance at the moment of one's buy fills is strongly negative, one is being picked off; the HFP benchmark (|Imb| ≈ 0.32–0.33) vs broker (≈0.52–0.57) gives a reference scale.
- VPIN's AR(1) ≈ 0.996 (50-bucket window) means it is a slow regime indicator for widening both sides, not a side-specific skew signal; markouts and fill imbalance are the side-specific signals.

### Gaps
- Andersen & Bondarenko's actual numbers (their correlation estimates, the timing of the VPIN peak) could not be read: SSRN and ScienceDirect returned 403; only search-snippet summaries are available.
- No primary academic paper was found that reports *a maker's own* fill-imbalance (buy fills minus sell fills) as a formal predictor with regression numbers; the own-fill evidence is markouts and fill-conditional imbalance.
- 2502.18625's reversal-classifier accuracy was beyond the portion read.

## KQ5. Explicit rules for translating flow pressure into price skews and asymmetric sizes (analytical and RL)

### Takeaway
The clearest closed-form flow→skew rule is Cotton's OTC result: with seller-probability q and market width w = 1/h, a flat maker should still shift its midpoint by δ = (w/2)·log(q/(1−q)) ≈ 2w(q − ½) (first order in imbalance) and widen by γ ≈ 2w(q − ½)² (second order); size rules are mostly implicit: control models reduce or withdraw the side flow is hitting (CDJ, Pulido et al.), and RL agents given size controls learn to shift lots to the side opposite inventory and to spread size deeper when strategic/toxic flow is present.

### Cited Findings
- **Cotton, "On a simple relationship between order imbalance, skew and width in OTC trading" (arXiv:2608.07690).** Poisson enquiries (mean inter-arrival τ), each a seller w.p. q (maker buys) or buyer w.p. 1−q; unit size s; adverse-selection cost ε per won trade; competing best quote with constant hazard h (width w = 1/h); carrying cost c(x). Define indifference-cost slope S(x) = [ν(x+s) − ν(x−s)]/(2s) (skew) and convexity C(x) (discretionary width); quotes: midpoint = fair − S(x), half-width = w + ε + C(x). Theorem 1: the imbalanced problem equals the balanced problem with carrying cost multiplied by M(q) = 1/(2√(q(1−q))), skew translated by δ = (1/2h) log(q/(1−q)) and width widened by γ = (1/h) log M(q). Expansions: δ ≈ 2w(q − ½), γ ≈ 2w(q − ½)², M(q) ≈ 1 + 2(q − ½)². Corollary 1: S(0) = δ, "skew is a flow phenomenon first and an inventory phenomenon second." With quadratic ν, S(x) = 2(C0/s)·x + δ (linear inventory skew plus flow intercept). A maker with zero adverse selection and zero marginal carry wins ≈ e^{-1} ≈ 37% of enquiries. No size rule (fixed s); no empirical claims. — [arXiv 2608.07690](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07690)
- **CDJ: side-withdrawal rule.** Post sell iff Δ/2 − ε⁺(J) + Φ⁺₁ > Φ⁺₀; in buy-heavy states the sell side is withdrawn unless inventory is far above the boundary; in sell-heavy states the sell is posted and the buy withdrawn (large no-trade regions, Fig. 6). — [ORA author copy](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:006addde-3a03-4d75-89c1-04b59026e1c0/files/me4008e0ecca779b45d59231ebca3e69c)
- **Pulido–Rosenbaum–Sfendourakis: size monotonicity.** q̂^a nonincreasing and q̂^b nondecreasing in y = efficient price − mid; sizes shrink with risk aversion and volatility, grow with MO intensity; negative inventory → buy more, sell less. — [ar5iv 2307.15599](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.15599)
- **Adverse-selection-adjusted half-spread (arXiv:2607.11888).** The Bayesian π̂_n enters the half-spread symmetrically via α̂_n; inventory skew comes from inventory terms, not from π̂_n; no size rule in the section read. — [arXiv 2607.11888](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11888)
- **Spooner, Fearnley, Savani, Koukorinis 2018 (arXiv:1804.04216).** State: inventory + active quote distances (agent state) plus candidate market variables (spread, mid move, book/queue imbalance, signed volume, volatility, RSI). Actions: 10 discrete — 9 (bid θ_b, ask θ_a) offset pairs in half-spread units including skewed pairs (e.g. ask 1/bid 3; ask 5/bid 2) and a market order of size −α·Inventory; **order size fixed (ω = 1000)**. Reward: asymmetrically dampened PnL r = Ψ − max[0, η·Inv·Δm] (η = 0.6 in Table 5). Results (HSBA.L out-of-sample, normalized daily PnL ×10⁴ mean ± sd): fixed θ=5: 2.80 ± 10.30 (MAP 2678); basic SARSA agent −12.45 ± 155.31; asymmetric dampening 13.88 ± 10.60; consolidated agent 15.43 ± 13.01 vs MMMW (Abernethy–Kale) 1.66 ± 22.48. Learned behaviour: the consolidated agent "targets a near-zero inventory"; the paper does not report that it learned to skew with book imbalance. — [ar5iv 1804.04216](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.04216)
- **Ganesh et al. 2019, multi-agent dealer market (arXiv:1911.05892).** Actions ε_b, ε_s ∈ [−1,1] scaling a reference spread per side (skew = asymmetry) plus hedge fraction; unit-size investor trades, **no size action**; 20 investors, GBM mid. Learned: with no skew bias in the reward, the agent lowers ε_b when inventory is negative (attract buys) and lowers ε_s when inventory is positive (Fig. 3; no numeric skew magnitudes); learned ε matches theory vs. competitors (−0.20 vs −0.25 theoretical against U[−0.5,0.5] random competitor; −0.03 vs 0 against U[−1,1]); excess PnL per step ×100 vs persistent ε=0: +76.2; vs adaptive γ=2 at 25% share: +92.5; against toxic investors it hedged more and outperformed the adaptive baseline (figures only). Drift μ=−1 → average inventory ≈ −16.06. — [ar5iv 1911.05892](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.05892)
- **Sadighian 2019 (arXiv:1911.08647).** State includes OFI across 15 levels per side (30 values) and trade-flow imbalance over 5/15/30-min windows; 17 discrete actions = asymmetric price-level pairs (offsets 0/4/9/14 levels per side) + flatten; **no size choice**. Coinbase BTC/ETH/LTC, train 27 Sep–4 Oct 2019, test 1–2 Nov 2019; average daily returns range −0.18% to +3.31% depending on reward/algorithm; trade-completion reward agents trade more and hold less inventory; no analysis of skew vs flow. — [ar5iv 1911.08647](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.08647)
- **Multi-level RL with explicit size allocation (arXiv:2608.18195).** Action = allocation of M lots (2 or 20) over a simplex of 8 components: unplaced, market buy, buy LOs at 0/1/2 ticks inside the bid, market sell, sell LOs at 0/1/2 ticks inside the ask (logistic-normal policy, Hamilton apportionment). State: best prices (returns), first K=3 volumes per side, mid drift, signed market/limit/cancel flows over the last interval, inventory, resting-order level/queue position/size. Reward: (1/M)(cash flow + MTM change − γ|Q|), γ=0.01. Learned sizing: noise market, M=2 → all volume at the best; M=20 → split between best and second-best; adding tactical traders → second-best used even at M=2; adding strategic traders → spread to the third level. Inventory skew: when Q ≥ M/2 the agent shifts volume to the sell side, strongly at M=2, mildly at M=20. Normalized cash flow (all three trader types, M=20): TOP1 0.73, TOP2 2.05, INV −1.44, learned 4.99. Fill rate falls from 74.65% (noise, M=2) to 43.99% (all, M=20). The paper does not report size as a function of the imbalance signal. — [arXiv 2608.18195](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18195)
- **Regime-switching flow (arXiv:2609.11614).** GLFT-style strategy's expected PnL peaks at balanced flow and turns negative once P(buy) deviates ≈0.08 from 0.50; a deep-RL maker stays profitable over ≈[0.40, 0.60] but shares the fragility. Belief state b_t = (ι̂_t = 2p̂_t − 1, expected run length) from a Bayesian online change-point filter feeds a Q-network with six tick-offset actions; skew is learned implicitly as Δa − Δb; unit lots (no size). Regime-aware fine-tuning recovers ≈80% of the PnL lost (paired, p<1e−4): stationary-trained agent ≈ −46% of symmetric-flow PnL under regimes vs +37% for the adapted one; inventory dispersion 1.89× vs 0.55×. Simulator calibrated to AMZN LOBSTER Aug–Sep 2025 (λ=0.06, μ=0.10, θ_cxl=0.02). — [arXiv 2609.11614](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11614)
- **Option market maker with asymmetric client intensities (arXiv:2511.02518).** Search summary states that when client buy orders are structurally more frequent the learned policy shows a wider bid spread than ask spread to discourage further buying; this section was not reachable in the HTML read (baseline symmetric λ± = 7560/yr). — [arXiv 2511.02518 (search summary)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02518)
- **Survey (Gašperov, Begušić, Posedel Šimović, Kostanjčar 2021, Mathematics 9(21):2689, DOI 10.3390/math9212689).** Frames MM as an MDP and concludes RL techniques give superior risk-adjusted return vs analytical strategies; no arXiv version. Full table of state/action spaces could not be retrieved (MDPI 403). — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/86c142702878470a833bb38d0424c979)

### Inferences
- Combining Cotton's δ ≈ 2w(q − ½) with CKS's β ≈ 0.45/AD suggests two complementary skew inputs: an *order-book* fair-value shift (OFI-based, in ticks) and a *trade-flow* skew proportional to the deviation of the buy/sell arrival probability from ½ scaled by the effective width. Both are first order in imbalance; width should respond only at second order (Cotton) unless toxicity (VPIN/markout) rises.
- Every size-aware source (CDJ withdrawal regions, Pulido et al. monotone volumes, the multi-level RL agent's learned allocations) points the same way: reduce or remove size on the side the flow is about to hit when the expected post-trade move exceeds the half-spread, keep or enlarge the other side, and push size to deeper levels when flow is strategic/toxic (markouts at the second level were 0.6–0.9 ticks vs ≈0–0.3 at the touch in 2608.18195).
- RL papers with price-only actions (Spooner, Ganesh, Sadighian, 2609.11614) consistently learn inventory skew but do not document a learned flow→size mapping; the only paper with explicit size actions (2608.18195) analyses size vs inventory and environment, not vs the imbalance signal.

### Gaps
- No academic source gives a calibrated closed-form "size on hit side × f(imbalance)" multiplier; the closest are CDJ's binary post/withdraw regions and Pulido et al.'s monotonicity (numerics only).
- Gašperov survey table (which papers use volume as an action) could not be extracted (paywall/403); Gašperov & Kostanjčar "Market making with signals through deep RL" (IEEE Access 2021) abstract also unreachable.
- 2511.02518's asymmetric-intensity numbers were beyond the portion read.

## KQ6. Online / Bayesian / exponentially-weighted estimation from a small, growing fill dataset

### Takeaway
The literature supports three estimator families that work on a maker's own fills: (i) MLE of the fill-decay parameter κ from Bernoulli fill/no-fill outcomes at each quoted distance, with regret O(ln²T) and |κ̂−κ*| ~ N^{−1/2} after N fills; (ii) Beta–Bernoulli posteriors for binary quantities (informed fraction, buy-probability) with closed-form updates and O(1/n) MSE; (iii) exposure-time intensity estimates λ̂(δ) = fills/exposure-seconds per distance bin with log-linear regression, which handles censoring of unfilled orders; plus filters (regime HMM filter, Bayesian change-point) for non-stationary flow.

### Cited Findings
- **Online MLE of fill decay with logarithmic regret (arXiv:2409.02025).** Ergodic Avellaneda–Stoikov: intensities λ^± e^{−κδ^±}, λ^± treated as observable offline, κ unknown. Each market order reaching the quote gives Y_n ~ Bernoulli(e^{−κδ_n}); log-likelihood ℓ_N(κ) = Σ[−κδ_n Y_n + (1−Y_n) log(1−e^{−κδ_n})], regularised by R(κ) = −κδ0 + log(1−e^{−κδ0}) and clipped to [K̲, K̄]. Algorithm: at each market-order arrival solve ℓ̃′_i(κ)=0, clip, and requote with δ_{i+1} = ψ^{(κ̂_i)}(Q_{t_i}) where ψ^±(q) = 1/κ + v̂(q) − v̂(q∓1); no separate exploration ("self-exploration" since one side is always quoted). Results: |κ_N − κ*| = O(N^{−1/2}) w.h.p.; performance gap ≤ C|κ−κ*|²; regret R(T) ≤ C1 ln²T + C2. — [arXiv 2409.02025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02025)
- **Beta–Bernoulli estimation of informed fraction (arXiv:2607.11888).** π̂_n = (a0 + Σ1{Z_i > θ_c})/(a0 + b0 + n); E[(π̂_n−π)²] ≤ π(1−π)/n + O(1/n²); credible width ≈ 3.92√(π(1−π))/√n; regret per fill ≤ C_α/√n; consistency relies on a monotone exceedance probability in π (asserted, proof sketched). — [arXiv 2607.11888](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11888)
- **Exposure-time intensity estimator (arXiv:2607.28323).** Λ̂(δ) = N_fill(δ)/T_exp(δ) per distance bin (ticks from mid), where exposure time accrues for every tracked order until execution/cancel, so unfilled orders contribute exposure but no fills (right-censoring handled by construction); fit log Λ̂(δ) = log λ − kδ. LOBSTER 2016 k̂: AMZN 0.48, TSLA 0.57, NFLX 0.86, ORCL 1.58, CSCO 3.72, MU 1.69 (per tick); FX (LSEG L2, 100 days 2026) k̂: USDMXN 0.52, GBPUSD 0.49, AUDUSD 0.54, USDTHB 0.62, USDSGD 0.61. No exponential weighting is used (pooled 252 days). — [arXiv 2607.28323](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28323)
- **Least-squares (A,κ) refit and non-stationarity caveat (arXiv:2609.11614).** GLFT (A,κ) recovered by least squares on an exponential fill curve; the paper notes long windows let mid drift make the intensity non-stationary and that unfilled probes are right-censored. Its Bayesian online change-point filter on market-order arrivals (Beta–Bernoulli segment model; posterior over buy probability and run length) is updated once per market order and summarised as (ι̂_t = 2p̂_t − 1, expected run length). — [arXiv 2609.11614](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11614)
- **Regime filtering from own fills (Campi & Zabaljauregui, arXiv:1902.01157).** Hidden finite-state Markov regime Y drives intensities Λ_i^±(δ); maker observes only her fills N^± and the reference price; posterior Π^i = P(Y=i | fills) follows Kushner–Stratonovich-type SDEs: between fills it drifts by regime transition rates and intensity differences, at each fill each regime's weight is rescaled by its intensity relative to the posterior-average intensity λ̂^± = Σ Π^i Λ_i^±(δ^±); optimal spreads depend on Π directly; full-information spreads are biased under partial information and "this effect becomes higher, the longer the waiting time in between orders" (i.e. sparse fills → more bias). — [ar5iv 1902.01157](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.01157)
- **Windowed imbalance as the standard practice.** VPIN uses a fixed rolling window of n = 50 volume buckets (1/50 of daily volume each), giving AR(1) ≈ 0.996 but bounded memory (no dependence on values 51 buckets back); the authors chose (50, 250) as "one week" and report that correlation with future |returns| increases with sample length. — [author PDF](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/con_035928.pdf)
- **Exponentially-weighted imbalance in simulators.** 2608.18195 defines an exponentially weighted volume imbalance (damping c) and a smoothed version that drive its tactical and strategic simulated traders. — [arXiv 2608.18195](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18195)
- **Micro-price data efficiency.** Stoikov states the Markov micro-price "needs less data to converge than averaging mid price changes over fixed horizons" and is horizon independent; G* fitted on one day (1 March 2011) matched the rest of the month out-of-sample on BAC and CVX. — [Stoikov slides](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~ajacquie/Gatheral60/Slides/Gatheral60%20-%20Stoikov.pdf)
- **Practitioner (confirmation only).** Hummingbot's Avellaneda strategy estimates intensity parameters from a buffer of recent ticks (buffer size 200 per the search summary); the hftbacktest GLFT tutorial calibrates A and k from recorded market-order arrivals ignoring queue position. The Hummingbot page itself returned 404. — [search summary; hftbacktest tutorial](https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/GLFT%20Market%20Making%20Model%20and%20Grid%20Trading.html)

### Inferences
- For a small, growing own-fill dataset the papers' structure maps to: per-level Beta (or Gamma-Poisson) posteriors for fill probability / intensity, updated at each fill or non-fill event, with the prior as shrinkage; and a Beta posterior on buy-vs-sell fill probability whose mean (2p̂ − 1) is a directly usable flow-skew signal (this is exactly the belief summary used in 2609.11614 and the Cotton skew input q).
- The exposure-time estimator is the correct way to use one's own unfilled quotes: exposure seconds per distance bin are data even when no fill happens; a maker who only counts fills will overestimate intensity. Exponential weighting of both numerator (fills) and denominator (exposure) with a common half-life is the natural non-stationary extension, but no source studied here reports such a scheme empirically — treat it as an unvalidated design choice.
- Campi–Zabaljauregui's bias result argues for widening/shrinking quotes conservatively when fills are sparse, because the regime posterior is then most uncertain.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports an empirical EWMA-of-signed-fills estimator with chosen half-lives and out-of-sample results; the EWMA appears only as a simulator feature (2608.18195) and as the VPIN fixed window.
- No source gives numeric guidance on how much shrinkage (prior strength a0+b0) to use for fill-intensity posteriors; 2607.11888 and 2409.02025 give rates (1/n, N^{−1/2}) but not prior choices.
- 2409.02025's numerical experiments (second half of the paper) were not read.
