# Market maker quote sizing by depth: multi-level ladders, backtested/simulated evidence

Scope note: this file covers (a) studies that compare ladder shapes on historical LOB replays or calibrated simulators, (b) RL market-making papers with multi-level action spaces and what their learned policies did, (c) agent-based / Hawkes simulators, (d) open-source practitioner frameworks' ladder defaults, (e) risk-budget arguments for total posted size, and (f) adverse-selection-driven shrinkage and requote timing. Units are reported as in the sources; "ticks" are exchange ticks unless stated otherwise. Where I computed a derived ratio myself I say so.

## Key Question 1: Which studies compare ladder shapes (flat vs. skewed-to-touch vs. skewed-deep) on historical data, and what won? Exact ladders and metrics

### Takeaway
Only three studies directly compare volume-allocation shapes across a fixed set of price levels. On LOBSTER replays (13 US large caps, March 2022, 10 levels/side) the only fixed shape with positive mean return skewed volume toward the touch (Beta(1,2)); flat ladders never had a positive mean return. In an agent-based simulator with 21 levels/side the ranking flips: with informed traders every multi-level fixed shape lost heavily, "deep" shapes (Beta(5,1)) lost least among fixed shapes, and only touch-only quoting was profitable. In a calibrated Poisson/queue simulator (3 levels/side) the learned policy concentrates almost all volume at the touch in noise-only markets and pushes 25-40% of filled volume to level 2-3 as informed flow is added. Net: the right shape depends on how informed the flow is, and there is no study on real data showing a geometric-decay or increasing-with-depth ladder beating touch-concentrated quoting.

### Cited Findings
- **Jerome, Palmer & Savani (2022), "Market Making with Scaled Beta Policies"** (arXiv:2207.03352). - Same paper, markouts per fill (Table 1, 30 s horizon): TOP2 (quotes one tick inside the touch, i.e. a narrower spread) has higher markout per fill than TOP1 (at the touch) in every market, e.g. all-three M=20: TOP1 -0.06 vs TOP2 0.59, but authors note deeper orders "fill less often, so higher markout does not guarantee higher profit"; "For TOP1, markouts with 20 lots beat 2 lots only in the noise market" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18195v1)
- **Law & Viens (2019/2020), "Market Making under a Weakly Consistent LOB Model"** (arXiv:1903.07222, published in High Frequency): "In practice, market maker will not put all limit orders at one single pair of optimal bid and ask prices as suggested by the AS framework; instead they will place a plethora of limit orders at many price levels in order to continuously maintain her priority in the LOB"; their simulated backtest (calibrated to QQQ, Nasdaq LOB, 8 May 2014 12-3pm) shows that ignoring price-direction consistency "may overstate the market-making profit by more than 50%" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.07222)

### Inferences
- The two shape-comparison studies disagree on direction because they differ in flow toxicity: on real LOBSTER replays (where the replay does not react to the MM and informed flow is whatever was in the tape) touch-heavy Beta(1,2) won; in the ABM with 52% informed agents, depth protected capital (Beta(5,1) > Beta(1,5) by ~2x in loss) but nothing multi-level was profitable. A practitioner should treat "how much to push deeper" as a function of measured markouts, not a fixed design choice.
- In all three studies, flat ladders (equal size per level) were dominated by some non-flat shape; the flat ladder is a reasonable default only when you have no information about toxicity.
- Cheridito & Weiss's INV benchmark (inventory-skewed sizes at the touch only) turning negative (-1.44) under informed flow is the cleanest negative result for "size skew without depth": skewing size at the touch alone rebalances into adverse moves.
- Standard deviations in the LOBSTER sweep (16-36 for touch-skewed shapes vs 5 for flat) imply that the touch-skewed shapes' edge is low-Sharpe (my calc: mean/std ≈ 0.036 for Beta(1,2)); the flat ladder is "safely unprofitable".

### Gaps
- No study found that backtests geometric-decay vs. linearly increasing size schedules (as in Hummingbot `order_level_amount` or Passivbot `double_down_factor`) on historical LOB data with queue-position-aware fills.
- Jerome et al. do not report Sharpe or inventory tables; ICAIF-24 reports payoff and spread but not PnL volatility or Sharpe.
- Chakraborty & Kearns (2011) ladder-profitability theorem could not be fetched (404); only secondary descriptions (ICAIF-24 related work, ABIDES docs) were available.

## Key Question 2: RL market-making papers with multi-level action spaces: what action spaces, and what size allocation did learned policies converge to?

### Takeaway
Most canonical RL market-making papers (Spooner 2018, Sadighian 2019, Ganesh 2019, Kumar 2022, ABIDES DQL-MM 2021) use a single order per side with fixed size; the multi-level works are Chung et al. 2022 (order stacking, KOSPI200), IMM/PIMMA (2 levels, N=20/side), Jerome-Palmer-Savani / Wang et al. (scaled beta over 10-21 levels) and Cheridito-Weiss (logistic-normal over 3 levels + market orders). Learned allocations consistently (i) concentrate at the touch when flow is uninformed, (ii) move 25-40% of volume one or two ticks deeper as informed flow increases, (iii) widen rather than flatten inventory when adverse selection rises, and (iv) skew size to the inventory-reducing side.

### Cited Findings

Consolidated agent (asymmetric dampened reward, LCTC state, SARSA(λ)) on HSBA.L: ND-PnL 15.43±13.01 (x1e4) with MAP 104±179 vs Fixed θ=5 2.80±10.30 / MAP 2678 and online-learning MMMW 1.66±22.48 / MAP 7330; Fixed θ=1 lost -20.95 — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.04216)

- **Resolving Latency and Inventory Risk in MM with RL (2025)** (arXiv:2505.12465): adds order hold-time to the (price, volume) action, 30-100 ms random latency, 500 ms batch matching; four real datasets; claims improvement over RL baselines but abstract gives no numbers or level counts — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12465)

### Inferences
- Across RL papers the recurring "learned ladder" is: ~60-100% of volume at the best price, the remainder 1-2 ticks deeper, with the deeper share rising with informed flow (Cheridito-Weiss Table 3) and inventory skew implemented as a side-asymmetric size, not as a different depth.
- Multi-level agents beat single-level agents in the only head-to-head on real data (IMM Table 1: DRL_OS and IMM vs RL_DS on RB/FU), but the gain is tied to queue-position retention rather than to depth per se.
- The reward structure matters more than the ladder for inventory: IMM's PnL-only reward produced 20x the MAP of the full reward; Cheridito-Weiss's static |Q| penalty cut std by 3x in noise markets but cost mean.

### Gaps
- Chung et al. 2022 (the canonical "order stacking" RL paper) is closed-access; exact number of stacked levels and size schedule unknown.
- No RL paper reports the learned size-per-level as an explicit schedule (e.g., 60/30/10) except Cheridito-Weiss's filled-volume shares; the posted (not filled) allocation is only shown in figures.
- Gašperov et al. 2021 full text (MDPI) returned HTTP 403; its action-space taxonomy table could not be extracted.

## Key Question 3: Simulation / agent-based studies (ABIDES, Hawkes) evaluating ladder strategies

### Takeaway
ABIDES ships a ladder market maker whose defaults are 20 levels per side spaced at 0.5x the EWMA spread with equal size per level set by participation of volume (5% of recent traded volume, min 20 shares) and sigmoid inventory skew of side sizes; the RL studies built on ABIDES (DQL-MM) and on Hawkes simulators (Kumar 2022) nonetheless use single-level, fixed-size actions, so the simulators have not yet been used to rank ladder shapes. The simulator-based evidence that does exist (Cheridito-Weiss Poisson/queue model, Wang et al. ZI ABM) is summarised in Q1/Q2.

### Cited Findings
- **ABIDES `AdaptiveMarketMakerAgent` (original abides repo)**: constructor defaults `pov=0.05, min_order_size=20, window_size=5, anchor='middle', num_ticks=20, level_spacing=0.5, wake_up_freq='1s', skew_beta=0, spread_alpha=0.85, backstop_quantity=None`; comments: `pov` = "fraction of transacted volume placed at each price level", `num_ticks` = "number of ticks on each side of window in which to place liquidity", `level_spacing` = "level spacing as a fraction of the spread", `backstop_quantity` = "how many orders to place at outside order level, to prevent liquidity dropouts" — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/abides-sim/abides/master/agent/market_makers/AdaptiveMarketMakerAgent.py)
- Same code, sizing logic: `qty = round(self.pov * self.transacted_volume[self.symbol])`; if `skew_beta == 0` both sides post `qty` (floored at `min_order_size`) **at every level (equal size per level)**; otherwise `proportion_sell = sigmoid(holdings, skew_beta)`, `sell_size = ceil(proportion_sell*qty)`, `buy_size = floor((1-proportion_sell)*qty)`; ladder prices: `highest_bid = mid - floor(0.5*window_size)`, `lowest_bid = highest_bid - (num_ticks-1)*tick_size` with `tick_size = round(level_spacing * window_size)` and `window_size` = EWMA spread (`spread_ewma = spread_alpha*spread + (1-spread_alpha)*last_spread`) — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/abides-sim/abides/master/agent/market_makers/AdaptiveMarketMakerAgent.py)
- Cofré & Mosionek-Schweda (2023), ABIDES bubble study (arXiv:2311.12247): the ABIDES market maker "places orders such that the difference between levels is proportional to the EWMA spread and the volume or size placed at each level is proportional to the traded volume"; "similar to the one in Chakraborty and Kearns (2011), but the ladder is built with a spacing proportional to the spread, instead of unit spacing"; their variant skews prices symmetrically around mid with inventory; the ladder's price-skew response to one-sided speculative flow is identified as a co-driver of the simulated bubble — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12247)
- **Deep Q-Learning Market Makers in a Multi-Agent Simulated Stock Market (2021)** (arXiv:2112.04494), ABIDES: background = 100 noise, 10 value, 10 momentum agents, 1 adaptive POV market maker, 50 investors; DQL-MM action = (buy ε, sell ε) ∈ 11 values in [-1,1] × hedge fraction ∈ {0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1} = 605 actions, **one price level per side**, order size unspecified; 250 two-hour sessions: DQL-MM mean PnL 159k (std 180k, min -148k) vs Random-MM -23k and Persistent-MM -37k; in a 3-DQL-MM market only one agent stays positive (129k; others -45k, -5k); learned hedge ε ≈ 0.2 (hedge ~20% of inventory per step), spread ε ≈ -0.2 single-agent and -0.4 multi-agent — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.04494)
- **Kumar (2022)** Hawkes simulator: see Q2; SYM-at-touch Sharpe 1.20 vs DRL 1.62, unit sizes — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.09951)
- **Cheridito & Weiss (2026)** simulator calibration (Appendix B): D=30 levels, noise-trader limit intensities by distance k from the touch λ^L,k = 0.2842, 0.5255, 0.2971, 0.2307, 0.0826, 0.0682, 0.0631, 0.0481, 0.0462, 0.0321, 0.0178, 0.0015, 0.0001 for k=1..13 (zero beyond), market-order intensity 0.1237, order sizes 1+half-normal(σ=2), tactical traders react to instantaneous imbalance with damping c=0.65, strategic traders to an exponentially smoothed imbalance (β=0.1); noise intensities scaled down 30% / 40% when tactical / strategic traders are added — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18195v1)
- **Lalor & Swishchuk (2024)**, "Market Simulation under Adverse Selection" (arXiv:2409.12721), CME ES/NQ/CL/ZN (Jun-24 contracts): a limit-order fill is "adverse" if the next best price moves through the order; naive posting (4 ticks from touch for ES/CL, 16 for NQ, 1 for ZN) produced adverse-fill shares of **81.5% (ES), 65.8% (NQ), 82.9% (CL), 88.8% (ZN)** of all fills; applying a non-adverse fill probability p=0.2 (vs p=1 implicit in Cartea et al. 2015) turned a positively-ending benchmark wealth path negative on CL and made the 330-path terminal-cash distribution "much worse" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12721v1)

### Inferences
- ABIDES' default ladder (20 levels, half-spread spacing, equal size = 5% POV per level) implies posted depth of 100% of recent traded volume per side; it is a liquidity-provision backdrop, not a tuned strategy, and nobody has published a ladder-shape sweep in it.
- Simulators that model fills as "price touches my level" (no queue, no adverse classification) overstate ladder PnL by >50% (Law & Viens) and flip the sign of naive strategies (Lalor & Swishchuk); any ladder backtest should report adverse-fill share per level.

### Gaps
- No ABIDES- or Hawkes-based paper found that sweeps `num_ticks`, `level_spacing` or per-level size and reports PnL/Sharpe.
- ABIDES-MARL (arXiv:2511.02016) has competing learning market makers but its action space and results were not retrieved.

## Key Question 4: Practitioner open-source frameworks (Hummingbot, Passivbot, Tribeca): ladder defaults, spacing, size growth, published backtests

### Takeaway
Hummingbot PMM defaults to a single level (order_levels=1, order_level_amount=0, order_level_spread=0) and lets users add levels with arithmetic size steps and arithmetic spread steps; its v2 `pmm_simple` controller takes explicit spread lists and percentage splits (equal split by default). Passivbot is a martingale grid: next entry quantity = position size x `double_down_factor`, spacing widened multiplicatively by wallet-exposure ratio and by a log-range volatility EMA, total size capped by `total_wallet_exposure_limit / n_positions`. Tribeca quotes one level per side (`size`, `width`) with hard position-divergence limits. None of the three publishes a backtest comparing level counts or size schedules; Hummingbot's docs explicitly warn backtests are for parameter sensitivity, not P&L prediction.

### Cited Findings
- **Hummingbot Pure Market Making (v1)** parameter defaults: `order_levels` 1, `order_level_amount` 0, `order_level_spread` 0, `filled_order_delay` 60 s, `inventory_skew_enabled` False, `inventory_target_base_pct` 50, `inventory_range_multiplier` 50 (as listed), `hanging_orders_enabled` False, `hanging_orders_cancel_pct` 10, `order_optimization_enabled` False — [Source](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/pure-market-making/)
- Hummingbot order-levels doc: `order_level_amount` is an **additive** step ("starting size of 7000 and a step of 1000, the second order is 8000 and the third is 9000"; negative values shrink deeper levels); `order_level_spread` is an additive percentage ("first-level spread of 1% with a 2% interval gives spreads of 1%, 3%, and 5%"); sample config BTC-USDT: bid/ask spread 1%, order_amount 0.002, order_levels 3, order_level_amount 0.002, order_level_spread 0.5%; warning that very small `order_level_spread` collapses levels onto the same tick after quantization; **no backtests or performance results on the page** — [Source](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/strategy-configs/order-levels/)
- Hummingbot inventory skew: allowable inventory range = target ± (total order size x `inventory_range_multiplier`); e.g. 10 BTC with 1 BTC order set and 50% target gives 40-60% at multiplier 1.0, 30-70% at 2.0; above the upper limit no bids are placed, below the lower limit no asks; order sizes "adjusted linearly" with the gap between current base % and target — [Source](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/strategy-configs/inventory-skew/)
- Hummingbot Avellaneda strategy: `order_levels` (default 1), `level_distances` (default 0; "How far apart in % of optimal spread should orders on one side be?", typically "tens or hundreds of percents"), `order_amount_shape_factor` (eta, default "Computed"; eta=1 gives symmetric sizes, other values asymmetric to reach inventory target faster), `risk_factor` gamma default "Computed" (common values 1-20), `inventory_target_base_pct` 50, `filled_order_delay` 60 s, `max_order_age` 1800 s, `volatility_buffer_size` 200, `trading_intensity_buffer_size` 200; the page gives no per-level amount formula — [Source](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/avellaneda-market-making/)
- Hummingbot v2 `pmm_simple` controller (dashboard tutorial example): `buy_spreads` [0.01, 0.02], `sell_spreads` [0.01, 0.02], `buy_amounts_pct` [50, 50], `sell_amounts_pct` [50, 50], `executor_refresh_time` 300 s, `cooldown_time` 60 s, `total_amount_quote` 100, `stop_loss` 0.03, `take_profit` 0.02, `time_limit` 2700 s; the page names backtest metrics (Net PnL, Max Drawdown, Total Volume, Sharpe) but shows no values — [Source](https://hummingbot.org/blog/dashboard-tutorial-pmm-simple-v2/); leaving amounts blank "distributes the amount equally" — [Source](https://hummingbot.org/blog/how-to-configure-a-v2-strategy-controller-in-hummingbot/)
- Hummingbot backtesting docs (Condor): backtests are to "understand parameter sensitivity and validate that your strategy behaves as expected—not to predict exact live results"; one-minute candles allow only one simulated fill per minute, so 1-second candles (Binance spot only) are needed for market making; "max active executors by level caps how many times you can replace a filled order, which effectively limits your exposure"; changing portfolio allocation from 2% to 10% "makes inventory build much faster" — [Source](https://condor.hummingbot.org/bots/backtesting)
- **Passivbot v7.5.0 `docs/configuration.md`**: `entry_grid_double_down_factor`: "Quantity of the next grid entry is position size times the double down factor" (example 1.4 x 0.9 = 1.26); `next_reentry_price_long = pos_price * (1 - entry_grid_spacing_pct * multiplier)` with `multiplier = 1 + (wallet_exposure / wallet_exposure_limit) * entry_grid_spacing_we_weight + log_component`, where `log_component` is derived from an EMA (span `entry_volatility_ema_span_hours`) of per-candle `ln(high/low)` weighted by `entry_grid_spacing_volatility_weight`; `initial_entry_cost = balance * wallet_exposure_limit * initial_qty_pct`; `wallet_exposure_limit = total_wallet_exposure_limit / n_positions` (examples TWEL 0.75 and 1.6); close grid: TP prices linearly spaced from `pos_price*(1+markup_start)` to `pos_price*(1+markup_end)` with "Quantity per order is `full pos size * close_grid_qty_pct`" (example: markup 0.01→0.005, qty_pct 0.2 gives TPs [101.0, 100.9, 100.8, 100.7, 100.6]); `unstuck_loss_allowance = past_peak_balance * (1 - unstuck_loss_allowance_pct * total_wallet_exposure_limit)` — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/enarjord/passivbot/v7.5.0/docs/configuration.md)
- Passivbot V8 (`trailing_martingale` schema, current master): `effective_threshold = threshold_base_pct × max(1, 1 + we_ratio × threshold_we_weight + vol_1h × w_1h + vol_1m × w_1m)` for re-entry distance; close threshold additive in exposure and volatility; v7 grid parameter names removed; default example config `total_wallet_exposure_limit = 1.5`, `n_positions = 7`; docs recommend `passivbot backtest` before live but publish no results — [Source](https://github.com/enarjord/passivbot/blob/master/docs/configuration.md); [Source](https://github.com/enarjord/passivbot/blob/master/docs/config_workflow.md)
- **Tribeca** (`src/common/models.ts`): `QuotingMode { Top, Mid, Join, InverseJoin, InverseTop, PingPong, Depth }`; `QuotingParameters(width, size, mode, fvModel, targetBasePosition, positionDivergence, ewmaProtection, autoPositionMode, aggressivePositionRebalancing, tradesPerMinute, tradeRateSeconds, longEwma, shortEwma, quotingEwma, aprMultiplier, stepOverSize)` — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/michaelgrosner/tribeca/master/src/common/models.ts)
- Tribeca wiki: `width` = "Minimum width of our quote in USD", `size` = "Maximum size of our quote in BTC" (single quote per side); `Join` posts at BBO unless BBO is narrower than width, then FV ± width/2; `Top` betters the BBO by a penny while respecting width; `PingPong` respects the width from the last fill; `tbp`/`pDiv`: "if you have 10 BTC to trade, set tbp = 3 ... pDiv = 1, your holding of BTC will never be less than 2 or greater than 4"; `apr` places "orders much larger than `size` and at prices much more aggressive than `width`" to rebalance ("a bit risky"); `trds`/`/sec`: after more than `trds` one-sided fills in `/sec` seconds, stop sending that side until the window passes or the fills are made profitable; "Tribeca is packaged with a backtesting mode" but no results are published — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiki/michaelgrosner/tribeca/Home.md)

### Inferences
- Hummingbot's additive `order_level_amount` and Passivbot's multiplicative `double_down_factor` are the two "increasing with depth" schedules in wide practitioner use; Passivbot's is explicitly a martingale (position-size-relative), so total exposure after n fills grows geometrically and must be capped by `wallet_exposure_limit`.
- The only volatility-adaptive spacing rule in the frameworks is Passivbot's `log_component` (EMA of ln(high/low)) and exposure-weighted multiplier; Hummingbot v1 has no volatility-scaled level spacing (Avellaneda strategy scales the first level via gamma/kappa only).
- Practitioner defaults for requoting after a fill are 60 s (`filled_order_delay`, `cooldown_time`) and 300 s executor refresh; these are far slower than the 500 ms (IMM) or 30 s (Cheridito-Weiss) decision intervals in the research.

### Gaps
- No framework publishes a backtest comparing `order_levels` = 1 vs 3 vs 5 or additive vs multiplicative size steps.
- Tribeca `Depth` mode semantics and `stepOverSize` are undocumented in the wiki; Passivbot optimizer bounds for the grid parameters were not in the fetched docs (example bound `long_entry_grid_spacing_pct: [0.005, 0.05, 0.005]` only).

## Key Question 5: Risk-budget sizing of total posted quantity from a capital/inventory limit and a volatility estimate (Kelly-style / variance-budget formulas)

### Takeaway
The cleanest closed-form sizing result is Kühn & Muhle-Karbe (2013): with exponential-type preferences the optimal inventory band is ±2·(half-spread)·(order arrival rate)/(risk aversion x σ²), i.e., the amount you should be willing to accumulate scales linearly with spread capture and fill rate and inversely with absolute risk aversion and variance. The Avellaneda-Stoikov/Guéant family fixes a hard inventory bound Q "depending in practice on risk limits" and sizes quotes, not quantities. No market-making-specific Kelly derivation was found; the Kelly/log-utility literature surfaced only for directional sizing.

### Cited Findings
- **Kühn & Muhle-Karbe (2013/2015), "Optimal liquidity provision"** (arXiv:1309.5235): small liquidity provider posts at best bid/ask (1±ε)S_t, mid is a martingale with vol σ_t, market-order arrival rates α^(1), α^(2); optimal policy keeps monetary risky position in [β_lower, β_upper] with **β̄_t = 2 ε_t α^(2)_t / (ARA(x0) σ_t²)** and β_lower = -2 ε_t α^(1)_t / (ARA σ_t²), trading back to the boundary after each fill; certainty-equivalent gain with constant parameters = x0 + (2εα^(1))(2εα^(2)) T / (2·ARA·σ²); with price impact κ the bands shrink by (1-κ) under symmetric flow — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1309.5235)
- **Guéant, Lehalle & Fernandez-Tapia (2011), "Dealing with the Inventory Risk"** (arXiv:1105.3115): inventory constrained to |q| ≤ Q with Q "depending in practice on risk limits"; asymptotic quotes δ^b(q) ≃ (1/γ) ln(1+γ/k) + ((2q+1)/2)·sqrt( σ²γ/(2kA) · (1+γ/k)^(1+k/γ) ), δ^a symmetric, so the inventory-driven skew per unit of inventory is sqrt(σ²γ/(2kA))·(1+γ/k)^((1+k/γ)/2); example parameters σ=0.3 tick/s^0.5, A=0.9 s^-1, k=0.3 tick^-1, γ=0.01 tick^-1, T=600 s; in their backtest γ was set "so that inventory stayed between -10 and 10" average trade sizes — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1105.3115)
- **Barzykin, Bergault & Guéant (2021), "Market making by an FX dealer: tiers, pricing ladders and hedging rates"** (arXiv:2112.02269): quotes are size-dependent mark-ups δ^{b,n}(t,z) over six size buckets (1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 M EUR) and two client tiers; objective includes -(γ/2)σ²q² with σ = 50 bps/day^0.5, γ = 2e-3 bps^-1 (M EUR)^-1, T = 0.05 day; optimal external hedging rate v* = (1/2η)·sign(p)·max(0,|p|-φ) implies pure internalization in an inventory band around zero; inventory-neutral 1 M EUR spread 0.26 bps (price-sensitive tier) vs 0.55 bps; internalization ≈ 80% for a risk-aware dealer; numerics cap |q| at 250 M EUR as a grid boundary, not a business limit — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.02269)
- **"Size matters for OTC market makers: general results and dimensionality reduction techniques" (2019)** (arXiv:1907.01225; author list not verified from the fetched page): extends multi-asset MM models "to include the existence of different transaction sizes" so the dealer "can quote different prices for requests of different sizes"; abstract only (no sizing formula extracted) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01225)
- Ganesh et al. 2019 risk-averse reward constants used in practice: -0.4·Std10(InvPnL), -0.07·InvPnL², 0.3·min(0,InvPnL), chosen "by trial and error so the RL agent still trades"; "a very large penalty makes it stop trading by widening spreads" — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.05892)
- Passivbot sizing from capital: per-position cap `wallet_exposure_limit = total_wallet_exposure_limit / n_positions`; first entry `balance * wallet_exposure_limit * initial_qty_pct`; re-entries scaled by `double_down_factor` and spaced wider as `wallet_exposure / wallet_exposure_limit` rises (`entry_grid_spacing_we_weight`) — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/enarjord/passivbot/v7.5.0/docs/configuration.md)
- Tribeca sizing from capital: `size` (max per quote) and `pDiv` hard band around `tbp` — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiki/michaelgrosner/tribeca/Home.md)
- A web search for Kelly-criterion market-making sizing returned no primary source; only general Kelly background and directional-strategy sizing (Scholz, "Size matters!", Frankfurt School working paper) — [Source](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:zbw:cpqfwp:31)

### Inferences
- Combining Kühn-Muhle-Karbe with a variance budget: if a desk tolerates PnL variance V per unit time from inventory, then |q|·P·σ ≤ sqrt(V) gives Q_max = sqrt(V)/(P σ) in shares, and the posted size per refresh should be ≤ Q_max - |q| on the inventory-increasing side. This is my synthesis, not stated in a source.
- The ladder's total posted size and Q_max are distinct: Cheridito-Weiss post M lots per 30 s but their inventory bands stay within ~1 lot (M=2) and ~6 lots (M=20), showing fills are a small fraction of posted size; sizing the ladder as a multiple of the inventory limit is therefore not necessarily reckless if cancels are fast.
- All formulas scale the band with 1/σ²; a doubling of realized vol should quarter the inventory band (K-MK) and roughly double the per-unit inventory skew (GLFT, which scales with σ).

### Gaps
- No market-making paper derives a Kelly / log-growth optimal order size; the closest growth-rate result is the K-MK certainty-equivalent, which is exponential-utility.
- "Size matters for OTC market makers" full text (how optimal quote depends on request size) not extracted.

## Key Question 6: How should sizes shrink as the book thins or fills become one-sided, and how quickly to re-quote after a fill?

### Takeaway
The quantitative evidence is that fills are predominantly adverse (65-89% on CME futures with naive posting; -0.8 bp expected 1-s markout at Binance BTC perp top of book) and that one-sided fills are the signal to widen or step back, not to add size; learned policies respond by widening the effective spread (shifting volume deeper) and skewing size to the inventory-reducing side, while static inventory penalties are ineffective. Practitioner rules are: stop one side after N one-sided fills in a window (Tribeca `trds`/`/sec`), stop one side when inventory leaves the band (Hummingbot skew range), and wait 60 s before re-quoting a filled level (Hummingbot defaults).

### Cited Findings
- Lalor & Swishchuk (2024): adverse-fill share of naive limit-order fills 81.5% ES, 65.8% NQ, 82.9% CL, 88.8% ZN over one active session; in the SOC market-making simulation for CL with non-adverse fill probability 0.2, 7,893 of 9,531 fills (82.8%) were adverse — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12721v1)
- **"The Market Maker's Dilemma" (2025)** (arXiv:2502.18625v2), Binance BTC linear perpetual, 12-19 Feb 2024, 232,897 minimum-size maker orders (127,051 filled): fill probability depends on near- and far-side queue sizes (≈30% when near queue large/opposite small; >90% when near queue very small/opposite very large); 1-s markout averages -0.058 bp (front of large near-side queue) to -1.157 bp (back of queue, both queues large); overall expected return ≈ -0.8 bp (-0.3 bp net of rebate); the naive maker strategy "lost almost 60% over about three days, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of -109.0"; "maker orders posted at deeper levels in the LOB requires different tools and is beyond the scope of this paper" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v2)
- Cheridito & Weiss (2026): the learned policy's cancel rate rises from 25.35% (noise, M=2) to 56.01% (all trader types, M=20) and deeper-level fill share rises to 8.30% at L3, i.e. the agent responds to informed flow by cancelling more and shading deeper; the inventory-skewed-size benchmark INV is worst (-1.44) under informed flow because it "rebalanc[es] during adverse price moves" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18195v1)
- Wang et al. (ICAIF 2024): "the optimal policy against the adverse selection may not be minimizing inventory but rather adapting swiftly by broadening its trading spread when risks arise and narrowing the spread when market conditions become more certain"; with informed traders the inventory-driven beta rule was second-most resilient (-4.18e7) after RL (-1.47e7) and far better than any fixed shape (-5.09e7 to -1.00e8) — [Source](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/97527/1/icaif24_29.pdf)
- IMM (2024): reward with inventory penalty cut MAP from 2156 (PnL-only) to 103 and raised normalized fill count from 4.43 to 5.15 per episode; re-quote cadence 500 ms — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.08918)
- Tribeca: `trds` and `/sec`: "If you do more than `trds` buy trades in `/sec` seconds, Tribeca will stop sending more buy orders until either `/sec` seconds has passed, or you have sold enough at a higher cost to make all those buy orders profitable"; partial fills count fractionally; `ewma?` suppresses quotes on the wrong side of a 100-minute EWMA — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiki/michaelgrosner/tribeca/Home.md)
- Hummingbot: `filled_order_delay` default 60 s (PMM and Avellaneda), `max_order_age` 1800 s, `order_refresh_tolerance_pct` 0; v2 `cooldown_time` 60 s after a stop-loss, `executor_refresh_time` 300 s; inventory skew stops bids above the upper band and asks below the lower band — [Source](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/pure-market-making/); [Source](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/avellaneda-market-making/); [Source](https://hummingbot.org/blog/dashboard-tutorial-pmm-simple-v2/)
- ABIDES ladder: `skew_beta` sigmoid moves size from the inventory-increasing side to the reducing side (`sell_size = ceil(sigmoid(holdings, skew_beta) * qty)`), with sizes floored at `min_order_size`; re-quote every `wake_up_freq` = 1 s — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/abides-sim/abides/master/agent/market_makers/AdaptiveMarketMakerAgent.py)
- Ganesh et al.: RL dealer "hedges significantly more than the adaptive agent" when facing a toxic investor and outperforms it; learned hedge fraction in ABIDES DQL-MM ≈ 20% of inventory per step — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.05892); [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.04494)
- Practitioner guide (non-peer-reviewed): measure adverse selection with post-fill markouts and "widen quotes when the 1-second markout falls below -0.3 times the current half-spread over a rolling 500-fill window"; responses listed are widen spread, reduce size, cancel one side, hedge sooner, stop quoting — [Source](https://www.quantvero.com/?p=139)

### Inferences
- A thin book (small near-side queue, large far-side queue) is exactly the configuration where fill probability is >90% and markouts are -0.5 to -0.8 bp (Market Maker's Dilemma Table 1): size at the touch should shrink or be pulled when your own order would be at the front of a thin queue against a heavy opposite side.
- The research cadences (500 ms IMM, 1 s ABIDES, 30 s Cheridito-Weiss) vs practitioner defaults (60-300 s) suggest the practitioner defaults are set for API rate limits, not optimality; no study quantifies the PnL cost of a 60 s re-quote delay.
- One-sided fills should trigger a spread widening / deeper shift before a size cut: both learned-policy studies and the ICAIF-24 text find widening beats pure inventory minimisation, and Tribeca's `trds` rule is a discrete version of the same idea.

### Gaps
- No study found that quantifies the optimal re-quote delay after a fill or the PnL impact of size-shrinkage rules on historical data.
- Markout-by-depth tables (fill probability and conditional return at 1, 2, 3+ ticks) were not found for any venue; the only markout-by-depth comparison is Cheridito-Weiss TOP1 vs TOP2 in simulation.
