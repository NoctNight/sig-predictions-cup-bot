# Theoretical optimal market making: quote placement and quote size by distance from mid

Notation used throughout (plain text): `s` or `S` = mid/reference price; `q` = inventory (signed, in units or in lots of size `Delta`); `delta^b`, `delta^a` = distance of bid / ask quote below / above mid; `sigma` = mid-price volatility (arithmetic, per sqrt(time)); `gamma` = CARA risk aversion (or inventory-penalty coefficient); `A`, `k` = fill-intensity parameters in `Lambda(delta) = A*exp(-k*delta)`; `T` = horizon; `Q` = inventory limit; `Delta` or `z` = trade size. All formulas are transcribed from the papers' PDFs (text-extracted and read directly); where extraction was ambiguous it is flagged.

## Key question 1: Single-quote models (Avellaneda–Stoikov; Guéant–Lehalle–Fernandez-Tapia; Guéant 2017 / Cartea–Jaimungal-type objective). Optimal quote distances as functions of q, sigma, A, k, gamma, T−t.

### Takeaway
In every model in this family the *size* of each quote is a fixed exogenous constant (1 share or `Delta`); only the *distance* of one bid and one ask from the reference price is optimized. The optimal distance decomposes as (i) a static "spread" term that depends only on the intensity shape and risk aversion (e.g. `(1/gamma)*ln(1+gamma/k)`), plus (ii) a term linear in inventory `q` with slope `sqrt(gamma*sigma^2/(2*A*k)) * const`, which is the inventory skew; in the finite-horizon AS expansion the skew slope is instead `gamma*sigma^2*(T−t)`.

### Cited Findings

**Avellaneda & Stoikov (2008)** — [AS 2008, DOI 10.1080/14697680701381228](https://www.math.nyu.edu/~avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf)
- Model: `dS = sigma dW`; agent maximizes `E[−exp(−gamma (X_T + q_T S_T))]`; bid/ask limit orders each for exactly **one share** ("is committed to respectively buy and sell one share of stock at these prices"); fill intensities `lambda^b(delta^b)`, `lambda^a(delta^a)` decreasing in distance. — [AS 2008 §2.4](https://www.math.nyu.edu/~avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf)
- Intensity derivation: from power-law market-order sizes `f(x) ∝ x^(−1−alpha)` and log price impact `Δp ∝ ln Q`, `lambda(delta) = A*exp(−k*delta)` with `A = Λ/alpha`, `k = alpha*K`; with power impact `Δp ∝ Q^beta` one gets `lambda(delta) = B*delta^(−alpha/beta)`. — [AS 2008 §2.5, eqs (9)–(12)](https://www.math.nyu.edu/~avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf)
- Frozen-inventory reservation prices (finite horizon): `r^a(s,q,t) = s + (1−2q)*gamma*sigma^2*(T−t)/2`, `r^b(s,q,t) = s + (−1−2q)*gamma*sigma^2*(T−t)/2`; mid-reservation price `r(s,q,t) = s − q*gamma*sigma^2*(T−t)` (eq. 8). — [AS 2008 eqs (6)–(8)](https://www.math.nyu.edu/~avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf); formula independently confirmed as "AS reservation price S − γσ²q(T−t), Avellaneda and Stoikov (2008, Equation (28))" by [Feys 2026, arXiv:2606.01477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01477)
- Infinite-horizon (discounted at `omega`) stationary reservation prices: `r^a(s,q) = s + (1/gamma) ln(1 + (1−2q) gamma^2 sigma^2 /(2 omega − gamma^2 sigma^2 q^2))`, `r^b(s,q) = s + (1/gamma) ln(1 + (−1−2q) gamma^2 sigma^2 /(2 omega − gamma^2 sigma^2 q^2))`, requiring `omega > gamma^2 sigma^2 q^2 /2`; the natural choice `omega = (1/2) gamma^2 sigma^2 (q_max+1)^2` bounds the inventory. — [AS 2008 §2.3](https://www.math.nyu.edu/~avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf)
- Exact first-order conditions (general intensities): `s − r^b = delta^b − (1/gamma) ln(1 − gamma*lambda^b(delta^b)/lambda^b'(delta^b))` and `r^a − s = delta^a − (1/gamma) ln(1 − gamma*lambda^a(delta^a)/lambda^a'(delta^a))` (eqs 18–19), with `r^b = theta(s,q+1,t) − theta(s,q,t)`, `r^a = theta(s,q,t) − theta(s,q−1,t)` where `u = −exp(−gamma x) exp(−gamma theta)`. — [AS 2008 eqs (16)–(19)](https://www.math.nyu.edu/~avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf)
- Asymptotic expansion in `q` with exponential intensities and a *linear* approximation of the order-arrival term yields `theta^1 = s`, `theta^2 = −(1/2) gamma sigma^2 (T−t)`, hence reservation price `r = s − q*gamma*sigma^2*(T−t)` (eq. 29) and total spread `delta^a + delta^b = gamma*sigma^2*(T−t) + (2/gamma) ln(1 + gamma/k)` (eq. 30), quoted symmetrically around `r`. "The bid–ask spread in (25) is independent of the inventory. This follows from our assumption of exponential arrival rates." — [AS 2008 §3.2, eqs (25)–(30)](https://www.math.nyu.edu/~avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf); spread formula also quoted as "AS-optimal full spread… Equation (29)" in [Feys 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01477)
- Simulation parameters used by AS: `s=100, T=1, sigma=2, dt=0.005, gamma=0.1, k=1.5, A=140`. — [AS 2008 §3.3](https://www.math.nyu.edu/~avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf)

**Guéant, Lehalle & Fernandez-Tapia (2013)** — [arXiv:1105.3115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)
- Model: `dS = sigma dW`, `lambda^b(delta^b) = A e^{−k delta^b}`, `lambda^a(delta^a)= A e^{−k delta^a}`, transactions "of constant size, scaled to 1" (footnote: "we can easily replace 1 by any positive size Δ"), inventory bound `|q| ≤ Q` (no bid at `q=Q`, no ask at `q=−Q`), CARA objective `sup E[−exp(−gamma (X_T + q_T S_T))]`. — [GLFT §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)
- Exact solution (Prop. 1–2, Thm 1): with `alpha = (k/2) gamma sigma^2` and `eta = A (1+gamma/k)^{−(1+k/gamma)}`, the functions `v_q(t)` solve the linear ODE system `v_q' = alpha q^2 v_q − eta (v_{q−1} + v_{q+1})` (boundary rows drop the missing neighbour), `v_q(T)=1`, i.e. `v(t) = exp(−M (T−t)) (1,…,1)'`; the value function is `u = −exp(−gamma(x+qs)) v_q(t)^{−gamma/k}`; optimal quotes
  `delta^b*(t,q) = (1/k) ln(v_q(t)/v_{q+1}(t)) + (1/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k)`,
  `delta^a*(t,q) = (1/k) ln(v_q(t)/v_{q−1}(t)) + (1/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k)`,
  spread `psi*(t,q) = −(1/k) ln(v_{q+1} v_{q−1} / v_q^2) + (2/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k)`. — [GLFT Thm 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)
- "the optimal quotes are almost independent of t, as soon as t is far from the terminal time T. This observation is at odds with the approximations proposed in Avellaneda and Stoikov using an expansion in q." — [GLFT §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)
- Asymptotics (Thm 2): `delta^b*_inf(q) = (1/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k) + (1/k) ln(f0_q / f0_{q+1})`, `delta^a*_inf(q) = (1/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k) + (1/k) ln(f0_q/f0_{q−1})`, with `f0` the eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue of `M`. Gaussian closed-form proxy `f0_q ∝ exp(−(1/2) sqrt(alpha/eta) q^2)` gives
  `delta^b*_inf(q) ≈ (1/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k) + ((2q+1)/2) * sqrt( sigma^2 gamma /(2 k A) * (1+gamma/k)^{1+k/gamma} )`,
  `delta^a*_inf(q) ≈ (1/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k) − ((2q−1)/2) * sqrt( sigma^2 gamma /(2 k A) * (1+gamma/k)^{1+k/gamma} )`,
  `psi*_inf(q) ≈ (2/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k) + sqrt( sigma^2 gamma /(2 k A) * (1+gamma/k)^{1+k/gamma} )`. — [GLFT Thm 2, Prop. 3 and following](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)
- Comparative statics: an increase in `A` "reduces the inventory risk… should have the same effect as a decrease in σ²"; dependence on `gamma` is "ambiguous" because higher `gamma` both narrows (execution uncertainty) and widens (price risk); spread is decreasing in `k`: `d psi*_inf / dk < 0`. — [GLFT §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)
- Extension with drift `dS = mu dt + sigma dW` (Prop. 4: ODE becomes `v_q' = (alpha q^2 − beta q) v_q − eta(…)`): approximation
  `delta^b*_inf(q) ≈ (1/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k) + ( −mu/(gamma sigma^2) + (2q+1)/2 ) * sqrt( sigma^2 gamma/(2kA) (1+gamma/k)^{1+k/gamma} )`,
  `delta^a*_inf(q) ≈ (1/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k) + ( mu/(gamma sigma^2) − (2q−1)/2 ) * sqrt( … )`. — [GLFT §5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)
- Extension with market impact / adverse selection per trade of size `xi` (Prop. 5): `delta^b*_inf(q) ≈ (1/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k) + xi/2 + ((2q+1)/2) e^{k xi/4} sqrt( sigma^2 gamma/(2kA)(1+gamma/k)^{1+k/gamma} )` (ask symmetric) — i.e. impact adds a constant half-width `xi/2` and inflates the skew slope by `e^{k xi/4}` (transcribed from extracted text; verify exponent placement against the PDF). — [GLFT §5.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)

**Guéant (2017), "Optimal market making"** — [arXiv:1605.01862](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01862)
- Model with general decreasing intensities `Lambda^b, Lambda^a` satisfying `sup Lambda Lambda''/(Lambda')^2 < 2`; trades "Δ by Δ" (fixed size); `dq = Delta dN^b − Delta dN^a`; two objectives: Model A = CARA utility `E[−exp(−gamma(X_T + q_T S_T − l(|q_T|)))]`; Model B = risk-adjusted expectation `E[X_T + q_T S_T − l(|q_T|) − (1/2) gamma sigma^2 ∫ q_s^2 ds]` (the Cartea–Jaimungal-type objective). — [Guéant 2017 §2, Thms 3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01862)
- Both reduce to one ODE system in `theta(t,q)` (`xi = gamma` for A, `xi = 0` for B): `0 = −∂_t theta + (1/2) gamma sigma^2 q^2 − 1_{q<Q} H^b_xi((theta(t,q) − theta(t,q+Delta))/Delta) − 1_{q>−Q} H^a_xi((theta(t,q) − theta(t,q−Delta))/Delta)`, `theta(T,q) = −l(|q|)` (eq. 3.9–3.10). Optimal quotes: `delta^b*_t = δ̃^b*_xi((theta(t,q) − theta(t,q+Delta))/Delta)`, `delta^a*_t = δ̃^a*_xi((theta(t,q) − theta(t,q−Delta))/Delta)` (eq. 3.14/3.16), where `δ̃*_xi(p) = Lambda^{−1}( xi H_xi(p) − H_xi'(p)/Delta )` (eq. 4.5). — [Guéant 2017 §3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01862)
- Exponential case `Lambda = A e^{−k delta}`: `H_xi(p) = (A Delta/k) C_xi e^{−k p}` with `C_xi = (1 + xi Delta/k)^{−k/(xi Delta) − 1}` (xi>0) or `e^{−1}` (xi=0); `δ̃*_xi(p) = p + (1/(xi Delta)) ln(1 + xi Delta/k)` (xi>0) or `p + 1/k` (xi=0); `v_q = exp((k/Delta) theta)` solves `−v_q' + (1/2) Delta k gamma sigma^2 q^2 v_q − A C_xi(1_{q<Q} v_{q+Delta} + 1_{q>−Q} v_{q−Delta}) = 0`. — [Guéant 2017 eq. (3.13)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01862)
- Closed-form approximations (valid far from T, independent of terminal penalty): `(theta(t,q) − theta(t,q+Delta))/Delta ≈ ((2q+Delta)/2) sqrt(gamma sigma^2 /(2 H_xi''(0)))`, so
  `delta^b*_approx(q) = δ̃*_xi( ((2q+Delta)/2) sqrt(gamma sigma^2/(2 H''_xi(0))) )`, `delta^a*_approx(q) = δ̃*_xi( ((−2q+Delta)/2) sqrt(…) )` (eqs 4.3–4.4). Exponential case:
  `delta^b*_approx(q) = (1/(xi Delta)) ln(1+xi Delta/k) + ((2q+Delta)/2) sqrt( gamma sigma^2/(2 A Delta k) (1+xi Delta/k)^{k/(xi Delta)+1} )` (xi>0), or `1/k + ((2q+Delta)/2) sqrt( gamma sigma^2 e /(2 A Delta k) )` (xi=0).
  Spread (eq. 4.8): `delta^b+delta^a ≈ (2/(xi Delta)) ln(1+xi Delta/k) + Delta sqrt(gamma sigma^2/(2 A Delta k) (1+xi Delta/k)^{k/(xi Delta)+1})` (xi>0) or `2/k + Delta sqrt(gamma sigma^2 e/(2 A Delta k))` (xi=0). Skew (eq. 4.9): `delta^b − delta^a ≈ 2q sqrt(gamma sigma^2/(2 A Delta k)(1+xi Delta/k)^{k/(xi Delta)+1})` (xi>0) or `2q sqrt(gamma sigma^2 e/(2 A Delta k))` (xi=0). — [Guéant 2017 §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01862)
- Sensitivities: `d delta^b_approx/dq > 0`; spread is affine in `sigma` and skew linear in `sigma`; "replacing Λ by βΛ is equivalent to replacing σ² by σ²/β" (liquidity up ≡ volatility down); in Model B an increase in `gamma` "is equivalent to an increase in σ²" (wider spread, bigger skew); in Model A the spread has a static term `(2/(xi Delta)) log(1+xi Delta/k)` decreasing in `xi` and a risk term increasing in `gamma`. — [Guéant 2017 §4 comments](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01862)

**Cartea–Jaimungal(–Penalva) running-penalty form and its link to AS** — [Feys 2026, arXiv:2606.01477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01477)
- CJ-tradition market maker "maximizes expected terminal wealth subject to a running quadratic inventory penalty and a terminal liquidation penalty", coefficients `phi` (running) and `alpha` (terminal). Feys shows the CJ framework is the second-order Taylor expansion of the AS CARA objective in inventory with the running coefficient forced to `phi = gamma sigma^2/2` (or `phi = gamma sigma^2` in the Cartea et al. (2015) convention without the factor 2), invertible as `gamma = 2 phi/sigma^2`. — [Feys 2026 abstract & §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01477)
- CJ-style reservation price and half-spread (in Feys's convention): `r(t,S,q) = S − 2 phi q (T−t)`, `half-spread = phi (T−t) + (1/gamma) log(1+gamma/kappa)`; at `phi = gamma sigma^2/2` these reduce exactly to AS eqs (28)–(29). — [Feys 2026 §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01477)
- Guéant's Model B (`xi=0`) *is* the CJ objective; its ergodic closed form is `delta^{b/a} ≈ 1/k + ((±2q+Delta)/2) sqrt(gamma sigma^2 e/(2 A Delta k))`, where `gamma sigma^2/2` plays the role of the CJ running penalty `phi`. — [Guéant 2017 §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01862)

**Fodra & Labadie (2012)** (AS with drift, terminal penalty `eta q^2`, linear jump approximation) — [arXiv:1206.4810](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.4810)
- Theorem 4 (ABM with drift `b`): `delta^{±}* = (1/gamma) log(1+gamma/k) + eta + (1/2) gamma sigma^2 (T−t) ± ( b(T−t) − q[2 eta + gamma sigma^2 (T−t)] )`; spread `psi* = (2/gamma) log(1+gamma/k) + 2 eta + gamma sigma^2 (T−t)`; indifference price `r* = s + b(T−t) − q[2 eta + gamma sigma^2 (T−t)]`; "if b = η = 0 we recover the results of Avellaneda and Stoikov". Closed forms also for OU mid-price via `E[S_T] = s e^{−a(T−t)} + mu(…)`. — [Fodra & Labadie §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.4810)

### Inferences
- Across AS/GLFT/Guéant, the *ergodic* skew slope `b := sqrt(gamma sigma^2 /(2 A Delta k)) * (1+gamma Delta/k)^{(k/(gamma Delta)+1)/2}` (Model A) or `sqrt(gamma sigma^2 e /(2 A Delta k))` (Model B) is the single number that converts inventory into price displacement: reservation mid ≈ `S − b*q`, bid at `r − c0`, ask at `r + c0` with `c0 = (1/(gamma Delta)) ln(1+gamma Delta/k) + (Delta/2) b` (Model A) or `1/k + (Delta/2) b` (Model B). The finite-horizon AS expansion replaces `b` by `gamma sigma^2 (T−t)` and `c0` by `(1/gamma) ln(1+gamma/k) + gamma sigma^2 (T−t)/2`; GLFT show numerically that the AS time-dependence is an artefact of the expansion and that the ergodic formulas should be used away from `T`.
- Size enters the single-quote closed forms only through `Delta` in `sqrt(gamma sigma^2/(2 A Delta k))` and the `(2q+Delta)/2` factor: the half-width risk buffer is `(Delta/2)*sqrt(gamma sigma^2/(2 A Delta k) …) ∝ sqrt(Delta)` (holding `A` = arrivals of `Delta`-lots fixed). This is the seed of the size-dependent ladders in Q2.

## Key question 2: Models where quote *size* is a decision variable or quotes depend on size; multi-level / ladder results and the shape of the optimal ladder.

### Takeaway
The rigorous literature does **not** optimize "quantity at each distance" directly. What it does: (a) OTC/dealer models (Bergault–Guéant; Bergault–Evangelista–Guéant–Vieira; Barzykin–Bergault–Guéant) make the *price* a function of the requested *size* `z`, giving a size-indexed pricing ladder whose offset grows roughly linearly in `z` and in `q`; (b) Guilbaud–Pham make limit-order size `l ∈ [0, l_bar]` and a two-level price choice (best vs. one tick better) joint controls, solved numerically; (c) Cheridito–Weiss (RL, 2026) allocate volume across three levels per side and find volume concentrated at the touch in benign flow but pushed to deeper levels as adverse selection increases. Inverting the dealer ladder (offset linear in size) implies a *flat* size-per-unit-distance ladder beyond a skew-shifted touch under the quadratic value-function approximation (inference, below).

### Cited Findings

**Bergault & Guéant (2021), "Size matters for OTC market makers"** — [arXiv:1907.01225](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01225)
- Motivation: "in markets organized around requests for quotes, quotes can and should depend on the size of the requests"; standard models assume "the same number of assets is bought or sold at each trade". — [B&G §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01225)
- Model: trades arrive as marked point processes `J^{i,b}(dt,dz)`, `J^{i,a}(dt,dz)` with intensity kernels `nu^{i,b}_t(dz) = Lambda^{i,b}(delta^{i,b}(t,z)) mu^{i,b}(dz)`, where `mu` is the size distribution and `delta^{i,b}(t,z) = S^i_t − S^{i,b}(t,z)` is the quote offset *for size z*; "Λ typically has the form λ_RFQ f(δ), where λ_RFQ is the (constant) intensity of arrival of RFQs and f(δ) the probability that a request results in a transaction given the quote δ". Inventory `dq^i = ∫ z J^{i,b}(dt,dz) − ∫ z J^{i,a}(dt,dz)`. — [B&G §2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01225)
- Optimal size-dependent quotes (Theorem 2): `delta^{i,b}(s,z) = delta^{i,b*}( (w(s,q) − w(s,q + z e_i))/z )`, `delta^{i,a}(s,z) = delta^{i,a*}( (w(s,q) − w(s,q − z e_i))/z )`, where `delta^{i,·*}(p)` is the maximizer of `H^{i,·}(p) = sup_delta Lambda(delta)(delta − p)` and `w` solves an infinite-dimensional Hamilton–Jacobi ODE; Lemma 1: `delta^{i,b*}, delta^{i,a*}` "are continuous and nondecreasing functions" of `p`. — [B&G Lemma 1, Thm 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01225)
- Numerical example: intensities `Lambda(delta) = λ_RFQ /(1+exp(α_Λ + β_Λ delta))` with `λ_RFQ = 30/day`, `α_Λ = 0.7`, `β_Λ = 30 /€`; request sizes `Gamma(4, 4e−4)` (mean 10,000 assets ≈ €1M, sd half the mean); optimal bid quotes "are increasing functions of both the inventory in asset 1 and asset 2" (positive correlation); quotes are reported "as a function of inventory and request size". — [B&G §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01225)

**Bergault, Evangelista, Guéant & Vieira, closed-form approximations with sizes** — [arXiv:1810.04383](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04383)
- Size-dependent optimal quotes (Thm 3, Model A): `delta^{i,n,b*}_t(z) = δ̃^{i,n,b*}_gamma( z, (theta(t,q) − theta(t,q + z e_i) + c^{i,n,b})/z )` with `δ̃^{i,n,b*}_gamma(z,p) = Lambda^{−1}( gamma z H_gamma(z,p) − ∂_p H_gamma(z,p) ) ∨ (−delta_inf)`; Thm 4 same for Model B (`xi = 0`). — [BEGV Thms 3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04383)

Resulting half-spread `(δ̆^a + δ̆^b)/2 = (1/2) sqrt(gamma) z_i e_i'Γ e_i + (1/(gamma z_i)) log(1 + gamma z_i/k_i)` (Model A) or `(1/2) sqrt(gamma) z_i e_i'Γ e_i + 1/k_i` (Model B); skew `(δ̆^a − δ̆^b)/2 = −sqrt(gamma) q'Γ e_i` in both. "These approximations give us a constant bid-ask spread and a skew linear in the inventory." Single-asset unit-size case "coincide[s] with the affine closed-form approximations… of Guéant, Lehalle, and Fernandez-Tapia". — [BEGV §3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04383)
- With distributed sizes per asset and tier (§5), the greedy quotes are `δ̌^{i,n,b}_t(z) = δ̃^{i,n,b*}_xi( z, 2 q' A(t) e_i + z e_i' A(t) e_i + e_i' B(t) + c^{i,n,b}/z )`, i.e. the argument is affine in both `q` and the requested size `z`. — [BEGV §5.4, eq. 27](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04383)

**Barzykin, Bergault & Guéant (2021), dealer market with hedging** — [arXiv:2106.06974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06974)
- Size discretised to the pricing-ladder buckets `z = 1, 5, 10, 20 M$` with probabilities `0.76, 0.15, 0.075, 0.015`; Figure 5 plots optimal bid and ask quotes (bps) for each size as a function of inventory: "accounting for size impacts the optimal bid and ask quotes. The monotonicity of the quotes is of course unsurprising"; the implied spread for $1M is 0.32 bps vs. 0.38 bps observed USDCNH interbank spread. — [BBG 2021 §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06974)

**Barzykin, Bergault & Guéant, "Market making by an FX dealer: tiers, pricing ladders and hedging rates"** — [arXiv:2112.02269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02269)
- Ladder streamed to tier `n`: `S^{b,n}(t,z) = S_t(1 − delta^{b,n}(t,z))`, `S^{a,n}(t,z) = S_t(1 + delta^{a,n}(t,z))`, sizes `z_k ∈ {1,2,5,10,20,50} M€`; flow intensity `Lambda^n(z,delta) = λ^n(z) f^n(delta)` with logistic `f^n(delta) = 1/(1+exp(α_n + β_n delta))` estimated on HSBC EURUSD data; `(λ_1..λ_6) = λ·(0.4,0.25,0.19,0.1,0.05,0.01)`, `λ = 1800/day`. — [BBG FX §2–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02269)
- Optimal ladder: `delta^{a,n*}(t,z) = δ̄^n( (theta(t,q) − theta(t,q−z))/z )`, `δ̄^n(p) = (f^n)^{−1}(−H^{n}'(p))`; optimal external hedging rate `v*_t = H'(∂_q theta + k q)` with execution cost `L(v) = η v^2 + φ|v|`, giving a "pure flow internalization area" `|∂_q theta(0,q) + k q| ≤ φ` in which the dealer only skews quotes and does not hedge; the area is wider for less risk-averse dealers, larger franchise, higher execution costs/impact, lower volatility; hedging rate "almost linear with respect to inventory" outside it. — [BBG FX §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02269)
- Figure 3 plots the optimal bid ladder `−delta^{b,1*}(0,z)` for all six sizes vs inventory (quotes for larger sizes lie further from mid; bid improves (rises) as `q` falls). — [BBG FX Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02269)

**Guilbaud & Pham (2013)** — [arXiv:1106.5040](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5040)
- Control: `alpha^make_t = (Q^b_t, Q^a_t, L^b_t, L^a_t)`, where `L = (L^b, L^a) ∈ [0, l_bar]^2 … represents the size of the limit buy/sell order`, and `Q^b ∈ {Bb, Bb+}`, `Q^a ∈ {Ba, Ba−}` choose between quoting at the best bid/ask or one tick inside (`P^{b+} = P^b + δ`, `P^{a−} = P^a − δ`); plus impulse market orders `ζ ∈ [−e_bar, e_bar]`. Fill intensities depend on level and spread with `λ^a(Ba, s) < λ^a(Ba−, s)` (priority effect); fills are of the *whole posted size* `L` (`dY = L^b dN^b − L^a dN^a`). — [G&P §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5040)
- Reduced IDE (mean-variance case) optimises jointly over `(q^b, l^b) ∈ Q^b_i × [0, l_bar]` the term `λ^b_i(q^b)[φ_i(t, y + l^b) − φ_i(t,y) + (iδ/2 − δ 1_{q^b = Bb+}) l^b]`, i.e. size trades off spread capture `(iδ/2 − …) l^b` (linear in size) against the concave inventory-value change `φ(y+l) − φ(y)`. Optimal policy depends only on `(t, inventory y, spread s)` and "do[es] not depend on the martingale modeling of the stock price". — [G&P §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5040)
- Stylized optimal price regimes (Fig. 3, plane inventory × spread): near zero inventory with wide spread quote `(Ba−, Bb+)` (improve both sides); with growing long inventory shift to `(Ba−, Bb)` (aggressive ask, passive bid) and beyond a threshold "SELL AT MARKET"; symmetric for short; the policy is "mainly time invariant near date 0" and gets rid of inventory more aggressively near `T`. — [G&P §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5040)

**Cheridito & Weiss (2026), multi-level RL** — [arXiv:2608.18195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18195)
- Prior analytical models "consider a market maker who only uses one limit buy and sell order"; Abergel et al. (2022) control prices of a fixed number of orders across levels "but their optimization method is computationally expensive"; Wang et al. (2024) use a Beta distribution to allocate volume across levels "but the market maker posts the same volume on both sides". — [C&W §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18195)
- Action: fractions `a_k` of `M` lots allocated to inactivity, market buy, limit buys at `k−2` ticks below best bid (`K=3` levels), market sell, limit sells at levels above best ask; modelled by a logistic-normal distribution. — [C&W §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18195)
- Results (10,000 test episodes): "For the market consisting of noise traders and for M = 2 lots, … volume is placed almost exclusively at the best bid and ask prices…, while for M = 20 lots the algorithm splits its volume between the best and the second-best prices." With tactical traders, "the allocations to the best and second-best prices are of comparable size, with a small allocation to the third price level." With strategic (informed) traders, "for M = 20 lots the volume is distributed over all three price levels, with a clearly larger allocation to the third-best levels… This progression is consistent with the increasing degree of adverse selection." — [C&W §7.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18195)
- Fill-level decomposition: filled volume "is tilted towards the best prices"; in the strategic-trader market 8.30% of filled volume (M=20) was inserted at level 3 vs 2.10% with noise+tactical traders; fill rate falls from 74.65% to 59.16% (M=2) as informed traders enter; markouts of level-2 (TOP2) orders beat level-1 (TOP1) but "limit fills deeper in the book are also less likely"; "placing more lots also means that more orders are affected by adverse price moves". — [C&W §6–7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18195)

**Law & Viens (2020)** — [arXiv:1903.07222](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.07222)
- "In practice, market maker will not put all limit orders at one single pair of optimal bid and ask prices as suggested by the AS framework; instead they will place a plethora of limit orders at many price levels in order to continuously maintain her priority in the LOB"; equal-size assumption "may mask the risk of overtrading" since "one large market order may raise her inventory to an unacceptable level". Their model fixes a participation rate `rho` so the MM's orders are "roughly the proportion ρ of the queue length at each price level" (size per level is *not* optimized). — [L&V §1.5, §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.07222)

**Abergel, Huré & Pham** — [arXiv:1705.01446](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.01446)
- FIFO LOB with Cox-process order flows; market maker strategies computed by MDP/dynamic programming with control randomization and quantization; abstract reports comparisons with naive strategies but states no closed-form allocation across levels. — [AHP abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.01446)

### Inferences
- **Size is a decision variable only in**: Guilbaud–Pham (continuous `l ∈ [0, l_bar]`, two levels), Cheridito–Weiss (RL allocation over 3 levels), and the dealer models in the sense that the *price for each size* is chosen (Bergault–Guéant, BEGV, Barzykin et al.). **Size is fixed in**: AS, GLFT, Guéant 2017, Fodra–Labadie, Cartea–Wang, Feil–Nendel, Barucci et al.
- **Shape of the dealer ladder in size**: with the quadratic proxy `theta(q) ≈ −(b/2) q^2` (`b = sqrt(gamma sigma^2/(2 A Delta k)) …` from Q1), `(theta(q) − theta(q+z))/z = b (q + z/2)`, so the ask/bid offset for size `z` is `c0' + b*(∓q + z/2)` (BEGV eq. 27 / Guéant eq. 4.3): **offset grows linearly in requested size with slope `b/2`, and the whole ladder shifts linearly with inventory with slope `b`**. Since `b ∝ 1/sqrt(A k z_unit)`, deeper (larger) rungs are priced wider in proportion to `sigma*sqrt(gamma)` and inversely to sqrt(liquidity).
- **Converting a size-indexed price ladder into a size-at-each-distance ladder** (my derivation; not stated in any source): if one posts one unit at the marginal reservation offset of each successive unit, the n-th unit on the ask side sits at `delta_n = c0'' + b*(−q + n − 1/2)` and on the bid at `c0'' + b*(q + n − 1/2)`. Inverting, the cumulative size posted within distance `D` of mid on the ask is `N_a(D) = floor((D − c0'')/b + q + 1/2)`, so the implied ladder is **flat: `1/b` units per unit of price, starting at a touch displaced by `b*q`**, with the first rung at `c0'' ± b*(1/2 ∓ q)`. Under the AS finite-horizon expansion the same holds with `b = gamma sigma^2 (T−t)`. Caveats: (i) this treats fills as sequential unit trades with intensity independent of resting depth and ignores that one large market order can sweep several rungs (Law–Viens overtrading remark); (ii) it ignores queue priority, which in Guilbaud–Pham changes the intensity by level (`λ(Ba−) > λ(Ba)`); (iii) Cheridito–Weiss show empirically (RL) that adverse selection pushes volume away from the touch, which the quadratic inventory-only proxy cannot produce.
- Guilbaud–Pham's IDE shows why posted size is interior rather than all-or-nothing: the gain is linear in `l` while the inventory-value change `φ(y+l) − φ(y)` is concave in `l`, so the optimal `l` solves a first-order condition `(iδ/2 − δ1) = −∂_l φ(y + l)`; with a quadratic `φ` this gives a size linear in (half-spread − b*q)/b — the same flat-ladder density `1/b` as above.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed/arXiv *analytical* result was found that gives an explicit closed-form "size at each level" ladder in a LOB (e.g. geometric vs. flat vs. increasing). The only multi-level allocation evidence is numerical RL (Cheridito–Weiss) and the two-level Guilbaud–Pham policy; Guilbaud–Pham's text sections read did not report the shape of the optimal `l*` (only price-regime plots).
- Abergel–Huré–Pham (multi-level price control) was read only at abstract level; its numerical policies were not inspected.
- Wang et al. (2024) (Beta-distribution allocation across levels, cited by Cheridito–Weiss) was not retrieved.

## Key question 3: Adverse selection / informed flow / short-term alpha — effect on placement toward or away from the touch.

### Takeaway
Drift/alpha terms shift *both* quotes in the direction of the expected move (ask wider, bid tighter if alpha>0) by `(mu/(gamma sigma^2)) * b` (GLFT) or `b(T−t)` (Fodra–Labadie), while adverse-selection-per-trade terms add a constant half-width (GLFT `xi/2`) and, in Barzykin et al. 2025, change the concavity of the value function: price reading (skew sniffing) widens the zero-inventory spread but *reduces* skew; adverse selection that is insensitive to the quote widens spread via `Delta_k` terms, whereas quote-sensitive (latency-arbitrage) adverse selection raises concavity and hence skew. Numerically, informed flow pushes volume off the touch (Cheridito–Weiss) and widens spreads in proportion to the fraction of informed traders (Barucci et al.).

### Cited Findings
- GLFT drift extension: bid offset gets `−mu/(gamma sigma^2)` and ask gets `+mu/(gamma sigma^2)` inside the bracket multiplying `sqrt(sigma^2 gamma/(2kA)(1+gamma/k)^{1+k/gamma})` — a positive drift widens the ask and tightens the bid by the same amount, independent of `q`. — [GLFT §5.1, arXiv:1105.3115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)
- GLFT market-impact/adverse-selection extension: constant extra half-width `xi/2` plus skew-slope multiplier `e^{k xi/4}`. — [GLFT §5.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)
- Fodra–Labadie: `delta^± = … ± (b(T−t) − q[2 eta + gamma sigma^2 (T−t)])`, `r* = s + b(T−t) − q[…]`: the reservation price is shifted by the expected drift over the remaining horizon. — [arXiv:1206.4810 Thm 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.4810)
- Barzykin, Bergault, Guéant & Lemmel (2025): reference price `dS_t = sigma dB_t − Σ_{n,k}[ ζ̃^{n,k}(S − S^{n,k,b}) dN^{n,k,b} − ζ̃^{n,k}(S^{n,k,a} − S) dN^{n,k,a} ] + Σ_n J̃^n( Σ_k w^{n,k}(S^{n,k,a} − 2S + S^{n,k,b}) ) dt`, where `ζ̃` (nondecreasing in the offset) is the post-fill markout (adverse selection) and `J̃^n` is the price-reading response of tier `n` to the displayed skew; sizes `Delta_1 < … < Delta_K`, tiers `n ∈ N`. — [BBGL 2025 §2, arXiv:2508.20225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20225)
- Baseline (no informational risk, quadratic Hamiltonian, exponential intensity `Lambda^{n,k}_0 e^{−kappa^{n,k} delta}`): `δ̂^{n,k,b/a*}(q) = 1/kappa^{n,k} + sigma * sqrt( gamma e / (2 Σ_{m,j} Delta_j Lambda^{m,j}_0 kappa^{m,j}) ) * (±q + Delta_k/2)` — i.e. the size-`Delta_k` rung is wider by `Delta_k/2` times the skew slope (extracted text shows `Λ_0^{m,j}/κ^{m,j}` in the denominator; dimensional consistency with Guéant 2017's `H''(0) = A Delta k e^{−1}` suggests the product `Delta_j Λ_0 κ`; verify against the PDF). — [BBGL 2025 §5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20225)
- Price reading "contributes to increasing the concavity of the value function… Skew sniffers detect this behaviour and push the price in a direction unfavourable to the market maker, thereby amplifying inventory risk"; the resulting first-order quote correction "implies spread widening in the presence of price reading" at zero inventory, while the tier-specific term "has the sign of −q on the bid side and +q on the ask side… contributes to reducing the existing skew"; "The second effect dominates, for instance, in tiers with a large proportion of skew sniffers that are highly sensitive to revealed information but trade infrequently." — [BBGL 2025 §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20225)
- Adverse selection sign depends on `(ζ^{n,k} Λ^{n,k})'`: clients trading on strong mid/long-term signals (ζ mildly quote-dependent) *reduce* concavity (flatter skew) because skewing toward flat inventory makes favourable informed flow more likely; latency-arbitrage clients whose `ζ` is steep *increase* concavity. When ζ is quote-insensitive, the local correction acts "according to the sign of q + Δ_k for bid transactions of size Δ_k and −q + Δ_k for ask transactions… the Δ_k terms contribute to widening the spread, while the q terms encourage additional skewing". — [BBGL 2025 §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20225)
- Numerical illustration: size ladder (1,2,5,10,20,50) M notional, daily vol 100 bp, baseline spread ≈ 0.7 bp; with skew sniffers the optimal skew shown to the sniffing tier is lower, "the de-skewing effect is particularly pronounced for top-of-book quotes"; for large sizes the sign depends on sniffer share (SVS=25% vs 75%). — [BBGL 2025 §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20225)
- Barucci, Mathieu & Sánchez-Betancourt: intensities `λ^a_t = ( φ e^{−k delta^a} [uninformed] + ψ e^{−k delta^a − γ(σ U_t ∨ S^−)} [informed] ) 1_{Q>q_min}` and `λ^b_t = ( φ e^{−k delta^b} + ψ e^{−k delta^b + γ(σ U_t ∧ S^+)} )`, where `U_t` is a mean-reverting fad; `ψ/φ` is the informed fraction. "When the price is temporarily above fundamentals (positive fad), informed traders sell more aggressively and the market maker optimally responds by widening the bid relative to the ask, even in the absence of inventory pressure"; "The bid-ask spread increases in the fraction of informed traders" and "increases when the running penalty on inventory increases"; the paper "extend[s] the Avellaneda-Stoikov expressions for the spread to be an explicit function of the fraction of informed traders". — [arXiv:2501.03658 §1–2, §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03658)
- Cartea & Wang (2020): alpha signal `alpha_t` jumps by `η^±` on market-order arrivals and mean-reverts; LO controls are binary `l^± ∈ {0,1}` at fixed depth (size fixed); post a sell LO iff `Υ_LO + h(t, α+η^+, q−1) > h(t, α+η^+, q)`; numerically "When the alpha signal is positive (negative), the strategy tends not to post" sell (buy) LOs to avoid being picked off, and uses MOs when alpha is large; profits from alpha increase with inventory-risk tolerance. — [Cartea & Wang 2020, DOI 10.1142/S0219024920500168](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:c2ba6656-8eab-4b2e-a24a-e9e842d1378f/files/s41687h481)
- Cheridito & Weiss: with strategic (informed) traders the RL allocation moves volume to levels 2–3 and skewing is "spread more evenly across order-book levels" rather than concentrated at the best ask; TOP2 (level-2) placement beats TOP1 for M=20 "because it is less likely to be picked off by adverse fills on the first level". — [arXiv:2608.18195 §6–7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18195)

### Inferences
- In the closed-form family, short-term alpha `mu` is handled by shifting the reservation price by `mu/(gamma sigma^2) * b` (ergodic) or `mu (T−t)` (finite horizon) — equivalently treat `q_eff = q − mu/(gamma sigma^2)` (GLFT) and reuse the inventory-skew machinery. For a ladder this shifts every rung on both sides.
- Adverse selection that scales with fill size (`ζ` increasing in offset, BBGL) raises the marginal cost of deeper rungs less than the touch, which is the analytical counterpart of the RL finding that informed flow pushes volume away from the touch; the inventory-only quadratic proxy cannot reproduce this, so ladder density should be reduced at the touch when markouts there are negative.

### Gaps
- Cartea–Jaimungal–Ricci "Buy low, sell high" (SIAM J. Fin. Math. 2014) and Cartea–Jaimungal–Penalva's book chapter with short-term alpha could not be retrieved (SIAM page blocked by JS); their formulas are represented via GLFT drift extension, Fodra–Labadie and Cartea–Wang.
- BBGL 2025's explicit first-order correction formulas (their eqs 12–13) were not transcribed; only their sign/structure was recorded.

## Key question 4: Inventory skewing — shifting price and size asymmetrically with inventory.

### Takeaway
Every model skews *price* linearly in inventory (slope `b` in the ergodic closed forms, `gamma sigma^2 (T−t)` in finite-horizon AS, `2 phi (T−t)` in CJ form, `−sqrt(gamma) q'Γ e_i` multi-asset), with the spread itself inventory-independent for exponential intensities; size-side skewing appears only in Guilbaud–Pham (asymmetric level choice and market orders beyond an inventory threshold), the dealer hedging models (externalise beyond the pure-internalisation band), and Cheridito–Weiss (volume shifted to the reducing side, and across levels when flow is informed).

### Cited Findings
- AS: `r = s − q gamma sigma^2 (T−t)`; "If the agent is long stock (q>0), the reservation price is below the mid-price, indicating a desire to liquidate"; the spread (30) "is independent of the inventory". — [AS 2008](https://www.math.nyu.edu/~avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf)
- GLFT: `delta^b − delta^a ≈ 2q sqrt(sigma^2 gamma/(2kA)(1+gamma/k)^{1+k/gamma})`; the exact asymptotic skew is `(1/k) ln(f0_q/f0_{q+1}) − (1/k) ln(f0_q/f0_{q−1})`, nonlinear for large `|q|` ("the closed-form approximations are satisfactory for small values of the inventory… quotes are not affine functions of the inventory as the closed-form approximations suggest" — BEGV/Guéant numerical comparison). — [GLFT Thm 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115); [Guéant 2017 §5 figures](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01862)
- Guéant 2017 eq. 4.9: skew `delta^b − delta^a ≈ 2q sqrt(gamma sigma^2/(2 A Delta k)(1+xi Delta/k)^{k/(xi Delta)+1})` (Model A) or `2q sqrt(gamma sigma^2 e/(2 A Delta k))` (Model B); `d(delta^b)/dq > 0`; higher `sigma` "increases the skew in absolute value". — [Guéant 2017 §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01862)
- Multi-asset: skew `(δ̆^a − δ̆^b)/2 = −sqrt(gamma) q' Γ e_i` — the inventory in *other* correlated assets skews asset `i`'s quotes through `Γ`. — [BEGV §3.3, arXiv:1810.04383](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04383)
- CJ form: `r = S − 2 phi q (T−t)` (Feys convention) with `phi = gamma sigma^2/2` forced. — [Feys 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01477)
- Inventory limits: no bid at `q = Q`, no ask at `q = −Q` (GLFT; Guéant; Feil–Nendel footnote "when q = Q, no bid quote is placed"). — [GLFT §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115); [Feil & Nendel §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17991)
- Guilbaud–Pham: optimal regime map switches from `(Ba−,Bb+)` at flat inventory to `(Ba−, Bb)` (aggressive ask only) as inventory grows, then to market sells beyond a threshold. — [G&P Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5040)
- Dealer models: within the pure-internalisation band `|∂_q theta + k q| ≤ φ` the dealer manages inventory only by skewing; beyond it hedges at rate `v* = H'(∂_q theta + kq)` that is "almost linear" in inventory; less price-sensitive tier keeps its volume share while price-sensitive flow is sacrificed as risk aversion rises. — [BBG FX §4, arXiv:2112.02269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02269)
- Cheridito–Weiss, conditional on `Q_n ≥ M/2`: for M=2 "the large majority of the volume allocated to the sell side…, and only a small residual allocation to the best bid"; for M=20 "the ask side of the book still receives the larger share"; "in the markets with noise and with noise and tactical traders, the sell volume is concentrated at the best ask, whereas in the market with strategic traders it is spread more evenly across order-book levels." — [C&W §7.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18195)
- Prediction market: "The skew is decreasing in inventory and vanishes at q = 0. Short positions induce positive skew…, long positions induce negative skew"; near settlement "there is less time to unwind positions before the terminal penalty is imposed, which leads to stronger quote adjustments." — [Feil & Nendel §4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17991)

### Inferences
- Practitioner formula set (ergodic, exponential intensities, size `Delta`): `b = sqrt(gamma sigma^2/(2 A Delta k)) * K`, `K = (1+gamma Delta/k)^{(k/(gamma Delta)+1)/2}` (CARA) or `sqrt(e)` (running penalty); reservation mid `S − b q`; bid `= S − b q − c0`, ask `= S − b q + c0`, `c0 = (1/(gamma Delta)) ln(1+gamma Delta/k) + b Delta/2` (CARA) or `1/k + b Delta/2`. Size skew is not produced by these models; a ladder built from marginal reservation offsets (Q2 inference) automatically posts more units on the reducing side within any fixed distance band, because the whole ladder is translated by `b q`.

### Gaps
- No closed-form rule for *size* asymmetry (e.g. "post x% less on the accumulating side") exists in the sources; only the implied translation of the ladder and the numerical RL evidence.

## Key question 5: Bounded/binary instruments (price in [0,1]) and terminal resolution with volatility growing toward the horizon.

### Takeaway
Feil & Nendel (2026) is the only rigorous treatment found: price = conditional probability driven by a transformed latent belief, quotes constrained to [0,1], running penalty on the *instantaneous variance* `q^2 ς(t,p)^2` (so volatility near 1/2 and near resolution matters), terminal penalty `−γ_T q^2 p(1−p)` for binary settlement; optimal quotes are the Hamiltonian maximisers of `Λ(π)(G(p,π) − z)` with `z` the discrete value-function slope. Numerically spreads decline over time but widen near settlement for `p≈1/2`, skew is nonzero even at `q=0` (asymmetric intensities), inventory risk vanishes as `p→0,1`.

### Cited Findings
- Setup: contract pays $1 if the event occurs by `T` else $0; market price `p_t = f(L_t)` with latent belief `dL = mu dt + sigma dW`, `mu := −a g`, `a = sigma^2/2`, `g = f''/f'`, so that `p` is a bounded martingale `dp_t = ς(t,p_t) dW_t`, `ς(t,p) = f'(f^{−1}(p)) sigma(t, f^{−1}(p))`. — [Feil & Nendel §2.1, arXiv:2607.17991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17991)
- Objective: maximise `E[ X_T + q_T p_T + Φ(p_T,q_T) − γ ∫_0^T q_s^2 ς(s,p_s)^2 ds ]` with `Φ(p,q) = −γ_T q^2 p(1−p)` (conditional settlement variance `Var(q_T Y|F_T) = q_T^2 p_T(1−p_T)`); "The parameters γ and γ_T allow the market maker to assign different weights to ongoing mark-to-market risk and terminal settlement risk." — [Feil & Nendel §2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17991)
- Optimal quotes (Prop. 3.2 / Thm 3.3): `π^{b,*}_{t,p}(z^b)`, `π^{a,*}_{t,p}(z^a)` with `z^b = (V(t,p,q) − V(t,p,q+Δ))/Δ`, `z^a = (V(t,p,q) − V(t,p,q−Δ))/Δ`, where `π^{b,*}(z)` is the unique maximiser of `Ψ^b(π;z) = Λ^b(t,p,π)(G^b(p,π) − z)` on `[0,1]`, given by `π^{b,*}(z) = 0` if `z ≥ u^b(0+)`, `(u^b)^{−1}(z)` if `z ∈ u^b((0,1))`, `1` if `z ≤ u^b(1−)`, with `u^b(π) = G^b(p,π) − Λ^b/∂_πΛ^b`, `u^a(π) = G^a(p,π) + Λ^a/∂_πΛ^a` (quotes clipped to [0,1]). Hamiltonian condition `sup Λ ∂²Λ/(∂Λ)² < 2`. — [Feil & Nendel §3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17991)
- Numerical intensity: `Λ^°(t,p,π) = A(t,p) B^°(t,p,π)`, `A(t,p) = (A_0 + A_1 (e^{ξ t/T} − 1)/(e^ξ − 1)) sqrt(p(1−p))` ("activity increases toward resolution and is maximal… p = 1/2"); `B^b = (2π/(π+p))^ν exp(−k(t)(p−π))`, `B^a = (2(1−π)/(2−π−p))^ν exp(−k(t)(π−p))`, `k(t) = k_0 + (k_1−k_0)(e^{κ t/T}−1)/(e^κ−1)`; "if k_1 > k_0, liquidity increases toward settlement, so… quotes must be placed closer to the current price to achieve a given fill rate." Volatility specification includes "σ_1 (t/T)^η capturing faster information flow near settlement" and "σ_2/(1+x²), which is largest at p = 1/2"; "Hence volatility increases as resolution approaches and is amplified when the event outcome remains most uncertain." — [Feil & Nendel §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17991)
- Results: "The spread generally decreases over time… Near settlement, however, the spread widens for prices close to p = 1/2, where settlement risk is highest"; "for any fixed time, spreads are larger for prices closer to p = 1/2"; "even without inventory pressure, profit maximization induces a positive skew for p < 1/2 and a negative skew for p > 1/2"; "Near the boundaries, the curves converge because risk vanishes as p(1−p) → 0"; versus a myopic benchmark the optimal strategy "substantially reduces downside risk at only a small cost in expected profit." — [Feil & Nendel §4.3, §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17991)
- Quotes are constrained to [0,1] and the HJB is reduced from four to three dimensions; existence/uniqueness of a classical solution proven via a fixed-point argument which also "yields a natural numerical approximation method with explicit convergence guarantees". — [Feil & Nendel §3.3–3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17991)

### Inferences
- For a [0,1] instrument the natural replacement for `sigma^2` in all Q1 formulas is the local variance `ς(t,p)^2 ≈ p(1−p) × (belief vol)^2` (plus the time-to-resolution ramp), so the skew slope `b` and the half-width should be scaled by `sqrt(p(1−p))` and increased toward resolution; the terminal penalty `γ_T q^2 p(1−p)` plays the role of AS's `(1/2)γσ²q²(T−t)` frozen-inventory term at `T`.
- Because fill intensities must vanish at the boundaries (B^b → 0 as π → 0), a ladder on a binary contract should thin out on the side facing the nearer boundary; the model gives nonzero skew at zero inventory for this reason.

### Gaps
- Feil & Nendel provide no closed-form approximation analogous to GLFT; all quantitative statements are from numerical solutions with illustrative (uncalibrated) parameters ("chosen to illustrate qualitative effects rather than to calibrate the model to a specific contract"). Size is fixed (`Δ`); no multi-level results.
- No other arXiv paper with an inventory-control model on bounded prices and multiple quote levels was found; the "Toward Black–Scholes for prediction markets" paper (arXiv:2510.15205) surfaced in search applies the AS approximation heuristically but was not read.
