# Mean reversion / momentum in prediction-market prices: arXiv sweep

Purpose: does exploitable mean reversion or momentum exist at hourly/daily horizons for a prediction-market market-making bot?
Source: HTML versions of each paper on arxiv.org, read via WebFetch (summaries by a small model; long pages were partly truncated, flagged below). Numbers should be spot-checked against the PDFs before being hard-coded.

Corrections to the brief (what the papers actually say):
- 2603.03152: the debate gain did NOT largely reverse. Trump YES +4c at 30 min, +2c at 3 h (about half remained). The paper's text is internally inconsistent ("gives back most" vs "about half"); the table supports about half.
- 2609.28965: "23 of 27" is 23 of 27 MARKETS (with enough observations) showing more negative stance before reversals, not 23 of 27 reversals.
- 2609.22639: actual title is "Adapting Pairs Trading to Gambling Markets: A Case Study of the U.S. Presidential Election" (Betfair exchange odds, not Polymarket).
- 2610.26789: the 417 markets are Polymarket BTC up/down five-minute markets, final five minutes only. It is not an hourly/daily study.

## 1. arXiv:2603.03152 Political Shocks and Price Discovery in Prediction Markets (2024 US election, Polymarket)

**Data.** Polymarket on-chain ledger (Polygon), 5 Jan to 6 Nov 2024. 3,654,974 matched trades, 232,469 addresses, Trump/Biden/Harris winner markets (plus Democratic-nominee market). Filters flag 2,176 advanced-operator addresses, 355 linked, 109 behavioral market makers. Executed trades only, no order book. Windows: +/-30 min in 5-min bins for trader response; 30 min and 3 h for price changes. Trade direction agrees with Lee-Ready on 96.8%.

**Shocks (UTC):** debate 28 Jun 01:00; assassination attempt 13 Jul 22:11; Biden withdrawal 21 Jul 17:46.

**Key results.**
| | Debate | Assassination attempt | Withdrawal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trump YES, 30 min | +0.04 | +0.05 | -0.03 |
| Trump YES, 3 h | +0.02 | +0.10 | -0.01 |
| Biden YES, 30 min / 3 h | -0.05 / -0.13 | -0.04 / -0.05 | -0.07 / -0.07 |
| Harris YES, 30 min / 3 h | +0.01 / +0.03 | -0.02 / -0.05 | +0.14 / +0.12 |
| Minted share of Trump YES supplied | 0.99 | 0.14 | 0.66 |
- Debate: the Trump move gave back about half within 3 h (reversion), while the assassination move EXTENDED (+5c to +10c, momentum). So the sign of the 30 min to 3 h drift depends on the shock type, not a universal reversion.
- Debate transitory price-impact component rises above permanent component as the move unwinds (Glosten-Harris decomposition).
- Withdrawal: 94% of taker flow in first 30 min reduced Trump exposure; 13 wallets sold $160,615 of Trump YES; top 5 reducers = 87% of reducing flow. Heavy flow, small Trump price move (-3c) because share minting let buyers/sellers match.
- Anticipation: switch probability (1 - Biden-nominee price) was 0.10 on debate eve, 0.65 on 5 Jul, 0.70 one minute before the announcement. Predicted announcement move -1.0 to -3.1c; realized -2.6c (30 min), -2.7c (3 h). Well-anticipated news = lots of volume, little repricing.
- Kyle's lambda (rolling, 2,016 5-min bins = 7 days) at the withdrawal stayed above debate and assassination estimates: the Trump book was not deeper then.
- Trader panel regressions (N = 284,420): prior volume/frequency positive and significant after all three shocks; coefficient table was hard to read in extraction (not captured).

**Actionable rule.**
- After a shock, do not assume reversion. Classify the shock: surprise with no linked-market confirmation (debate-type) tends to partially retrace within hours; shock with confirming move in linked markets and new-address entry (assassination-type) tends to continue.
- Judge belief revision from linked-market prices (Biden/Harris/Trump legs), not from volume or new addresses.
- Widen quotes and cut size on the side facing informed flow in the first 30 min; Kyle's lambda is elevated and flow is one-sided (94% one-way at withdrawal).
- Discount volume spikes when the event was priced in (switch-prob 0.65-0.70 before the news).

**Caveats.** One market, one election, three events. Executed trades only. Short windows (30 min / 3 h) say nothing about daily reversion. Heuristic wallet filters. Observational, no causal test. Paper wording on the debate reversal is inconsistent. Last ~20k characters of the HTML were not read.

## 2. arXiv:2609.28965 The Conversation Turns First: Crowd Discussion and Price Reversals (non-political Polymarket)

**Data.** Non-political single-market Polymarket events with >100 comments: 79 markets in correlation sweep, 76 in six-hour panel (18,399 six-hour blocks), 63,720 scored comments. Stance/sentiment/toxicity labels generated by an LLM from resolution criteria. Evaluation mostly leave-one-market-out. Sweep: 34,938,540 grid cells, 2,388,347 computable; six-hour bins cleared most often (7,775 cells vs 6,334 at 12 h and 4,503 at 24 h).

**Key results.**
- Heavy trading in next 18 h (live commented blocks; 2,462 blocks, 65 markets, prevalence 0.606): PR-AUC attention 0.786, trading history 0.790, combined 0.793. Accuracy at 0.5: attention 0.612, trading 0.685, majority 0.606. Top decile precision: attention 0.745, combined 0.833. So attention is about as good as trading history, not better.
- Short markets (median 15 d, 15.1 comments/day): attention 0.766 vs trading 0.718. Long markets (median 76.5 d): trading 0.833 vs attention 0.798; top-decile trading precision 0.975.
- Direction of buying (53 markets): flow history alone ROC-AUC 0.780 (acc 0.805); comments alone 0.540-0.586 (toxicity 0.586, stance 0.580, attention 0.571); flow + comments 0.788. Comments add almost nothing over order-flow persistence (i.e. flow is strongly autocorrelated at 6 h = momentum in order flow). Daily aggregation: stance 0.547, flow 0.805.
- Activity increase next block: trading PR-AUC 0.734 +/- 0.027 on unseen markets (majority 0.516).
- Leadership change within 4 blocks (24 h), prevalence 0.078: pooled PR-AUC 0.403, ROC-AUC 0.868. Market-state model (distance from even odds, liveness, recent movement) PR-AUC 0.515 on 20-market holdout; price distance alone 0.511; comments alone 0.158. At 0.5 threshold: precision 0.548, recall 0.287, lift 7.1; top decile precision 0.402, recall 0.518.
- Stance before reversals: mean stance to the leader -0.080 in day before reversal vs +0.025 otherwise; 23 of 27 markets showed more negative stance before reversal (binomial p ~ 0.0003; typical within-market gap 0.235). But stance alone PR-AUC 0.121 vs prevalence 0.101, ROC-AUC 0.486; adding stance to market state 0.394 -> 0.414 (smaller than fold SD 0.109).

**Actionable rule.**
- Leader flips are predicted by price state (close to 50%, live, recently moving), not by sentiment. Treat markets near even odds with recent movement as high reversal-risk (lift 5-7x) and widen/shrink quotes there.
- Use order-flow imbalance persistence (6 h blocks) as the direction/inventory-risk signal; comments are optional and weak.
- Comment-count/attention is a cheap proxy for an upcoming volume burst in short (< ~3 week) markets; use it for quote-size/uptime planning, not for direction.

**Caveats.** Associational; exploratory sweep with dependent observations and no permutation null; evaluated markets informed setting selection; non-political, discussion-rich markets only; LLM labels unvalidated per comment; no returns, no costs, no fills tested; good rankings did not give calibrated 0.5-threshold decisions. Stance as a stand-alone predictor is useless.

## 3. arXiv:2609.22639 Adapting Pairs Trading to Gambling Markets: Case Study of the US Presidential Election (Betfair, latent OU)

**Data.** Betfair exchange odds (Betdata archive), 5-minute sampling. Train: 2020 Trump vs Biden, 21,181 obs after cleaning (first 10,000 seed fit). Out-of-sample test: 2024 Trump vs Harris, 21,689 obs, 23 Aug to 6 Nov. NOT Polymarket.

**Model.** Combined implied probability of the two nominees = trending Ornstein-Uhlenbeck with additive white noise and time-varying mean (penalized spline mean + Kalman-filter MLE). Flat repeated quotes collapsed to one obs.

**Parameters.**
| | 2020 train | 2024 test |
|---|---|---|
| theta (reversion rate) | 0.134 | 0.181 |
| sigma^2 | 4.83e-6 | 4.15e-6 |
| omega^2 (noise) | 2.73e-6 | 2.35e-6 |
| long-run mean | 0.988 | 0.985 |
- Half-life ln2/theta: about 5.2 intervals (2020), about 3.8 intervals (2024) = about 19 min if theta is per 5-min step (unit not confirmed; my calculation, not the paper's).
- No formal stationarity/unit-root test reported.

**Trading rule and results.** Bootstrap one-sided upper prediction bound on next combined probability; signal when current value exceeds it; Bradley-Terry-type model picks which candidate; exit at first change in the selected quote; one position at a time. 2024: 130 signals, all completed, coverage 95.1% vs 95% target. Mean synthetic odds-price return 1.86% per trade, per-trade Sharpe-type ratio 1.12 (not annualized). 2020: 107 signals (with COVID) / 201 (excluding).

**Interpretation (my inference).** The reverting quantity is the SUM of the two nominees' implied probabilities (long-run mean about 0.985-0.988, i.e. close to 1 minus overround), not an individual outcome probability. A roughly 20-minute half-life on a sum near 1 looks like quote-level/microstructure mean reversion (bid-ask bounce, stale quotes, leg desync), which is exactly what a market maker already harvests, not hourly/daily directional reversion.

**Actionable rule.**
- Monitor the sum of complementary legs (YES+NO, or nominee A + nominee B in a two-horse market): deviations from the equilibrium level revert within minutes; quote around the fair sum, lean inventory against the deviation.
- Do not read this as evidence of hourly/daily mean reversion in the probability itself.

**Caveats.** Returns are frictionless changes in decimal odds; no commission, spread, queue, partial fill, or lay liability. Self-described proof of concept; two elections; residual non-normality and serial dependence (negative lag-1 ACF in 2024 residuals, consistent with bounce); missing-completely-at-random assumption doubtful in disrupted periods; variable holding period. Last ~42k chars of HTML not read.

## 4. arXiv:2602.19520 Decomposing Crowd Wisdom: Domain-Specific Calibration Dynamics in Prediction Markets

**Data.** 353M trades, about 429,000 binary contracts, to 31 Dec 2025. Kalshi (primary): 64.7M trades, 210,608 markets, about 16.8B contracts. Polymarket: 288.7M trades, about 218,000 resolved contracts, 79.8B contracts (Sports, Crypto, Politics only). Calibration sample after 5-95c filter: 58.7M Kalshi, 135.6M Polymarket trades. Logistic recalibration slope (>1 = prices compressed toward 50%, underconfident; <1 = too extreme). Grid 6 domains x 9 time-to-resolution bins x 4 trade-size bins = 216 cells.

**Key results (Kalshi slopes).**
- Politics: 0.93-1.83 across horizons; underconfident at almost every horizon (minimum 0.93 at 1-3 h). Illustration: a 70c political price one week out maps to about 83% with slope 1.83 (paper says use the intercept-aware formula in practice).
- Sports: 0.90-1.10 out to 48 h, 1.74 at 1 month+. Crypto 0.99-1.36. Finance 0.82-1.42. Weather 0.69-0.97 within 48 h (too extreme), 1.37 at 1 month+. Entertainment 0.81-1.11.
- Cross-domain mean slope rises from 0.99 (0-1 h) to 1.32 (beyond 1 month): long-dated prices are too compressed; near-expiry prices are about calibrated.
- Politics intercept +0.107 (CrI 0.062-0.152); weather -0.072; entertainment -0.062. Politics ECE 0.117 vs <= 0.022 elsewhere.
- Trade size, Kalshi politics: large trades (>100 contracts) slope 1.74 vs 1.19 single-contract; gap 0.53 (trade-level CI [0.29, 0.75]; market-clustered [0.14, 1.32]; event-clustered [0.12, 1.80]). Kalshi sports gap 0.07, null.
- Polymarket mean slopes over seven reliable bins: Politics 1.451 (Kalshi 1.608), Sports 1.059, Crypto 1.056. Politics by bin: 3-6 h 1.514, 6-12 h 1.216, 12-24 h 1.392, 24-48 h 1.204, 2 d-1 w 2.077, 1 w-1 mo 1.671, 1 mo+ 1.083. 0-1 h and 1-3 h bins unreliable (timestamps have about 3 h noise).
- Polymarket trade-size effect politics +0.182 (single 1.211, large 1.393); cell-level +0.281 CI [0.026, 0.542], market-clustered +0.210 CI [-0.311, 1.122] (not significant): size effect is Kalshi-specific.
- Variance explained: in-sample R^2 87.3% (adj 0.810, 72 params); leave-one-cell-out R^2 0.715; 10-fold 0.704; permutation null mean 0.329. Roughly half of raw slope variation is estimation noise; structural components about 45.6% (CrI 31-62%).
- No trading or return analysis in the paper.

**Actionable rule.**
- Static bias, not dynamic mean reversion: in political markets (and any long-dated market) the fair value is further from 50% than the quote. Skew the quoted midpoint away from 50% in political markets, scaling with time to resolution (slope about 1.2-1.8 on Kalshi, 1.2-1.5 on Polymarket), and keep it neutral for sports/crypto inside 48 h.
- Never apply a slope-only recalibration; fit slope and intercept per domain/horizon and shrink hard (half of the slope dispersion is noise).
- Horizon profile of the Polymarket politics slope is non-monotonic (3-6 h 1.51, 24-48 h 1.20, 2 d-1 w 2.08, 1 w-1 mo 1.67, 1 mo+ 1.08), so use bin-specific shrunk estimates rather than a smooth extrapolation.
- Treat large-trade flow in Kalshi politics as more informative (higher slope), but not on Polymarket.

**Caveats.** Descriptive, not causal; slope is a calibration statistic measured at trade prices, not a tradable edge net of spread and fees; Polymarket timestamps coarse (about 3 h), regex domain classifier leaves many markets in "Other"; Polymarket snapshot differs from original submission; the discussion/limitations sections (last ~27k chars) were not read; 2024 election daily markets may drive the Polymarket 2 d-1 w spike.

## 5. arXiv:2610.26789 Cooperative Multi-Agent RL for Market Making on Prediction-Market Order Books

**Data.** Polymarket BTC up/down five-minute binary markets, final five minutes only, L2 book updates converted to LOBSTER-format order data (synthetic order IDs/queue positions). 417 markets in the data pipeline; training on 4 days (22 and 28 Apr, 14 and 28 May 2026, 640 streams), held-out day 11 Jun 2026 (192 streams), 12:00-20:00 UTC. No transaction costs, no price impact from agents.

**Method.** JaxMARL-HFT / JAX-LOB replay, PPO + GRU(256), 3.2M env steps, one seed per configuration. Desk = quoter + risk-manager agents with reward shaping (blend 0.3, markout 0.3 at horizon 5 or 0.15 ramped, exit credit 0.01). Inventory cap 4 (20 for baseline), annealed loss stop.

**Held-out portfolio value (ticks), mean of last five evals:**
| Config | PV | SD | Volume/step | No-trade | Mean abs inventory |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PAPER_BASE | -46.1 | 19.4 | 0.056 | 19.2% | 1.108 |
| WIDE_SPOONER (single-agent redesign) | -9.6 | 3.1 | 0.032 | 4.4% | 0.366 |
| D_SPLIT | -72.3 | 17.6 | 0.112 | 1.6% | 0.241 |
| D_BLEND | -41.0 | 12.3 | 0.068 | 4.7% | 0.172 |
| D_MARK | -5.9 | 3.5 | 0.032 | 20.4% | 0.082 |
| D_BL_MARK_CURR | -4.3 | 1.7 | 0.022 | 19.7% | 0.060 |
- Baseline no-trade rate 61.1%-97.7% across three seeds in the earlier (base) setup: replayed flow pushes the learner to stop quoting.
- 48-window view: D_SPLIT loses in 45 windows, D_BLEND 39, D_MARK 6 (total +133 ticks), D_BL_MARK_CURR 8 (+67 ticks). Authors state positive deterministic-window totals do not establish positive expected profit; all configurations have negative mean PV.
- Markout in the reward is the main fix: reducing the quoter's adverse selection takes PV from -72 to about -5.

**Actionable rule.**
- Passive two-sided quoting on real Polymarket flow loses to adverse selection; the best learned policy only gets to about breakeven by quoting rarely and tiny (volume 0.022-0.032, mean |inventory| 0.06-0.08).
- Score every quoting rule on markout (post-fill mid change, ~5 steps) before PnL; kill rules with negative markout.
- Use hard inventory caps and loss stops (cap 4 units, annealed stop) as risk controls rather than reward penalties; allow explicit no-quote.
- Do not trust an RL market maker as the source of alpha.

**Caveats.** Five-minute BTC markets in the last 5 minutes, which is a very different regime from hourly/daily political markets. One held-out day, one seed per configuration, dispersion is across windows not seeds, learned execution counterparty (non-stationary), synthetic queue positions, zero fees, zero impact. Units are ticks, not dollars. Gains are relative to the authors' own retrained baseline. Abstract's "no learned market maker achieves positive average portfolio value" is confirmed by Tables 6-7 (all PV negative).

## What this means for the bot

Bottom line: the five papers do NOT show an exploitable hourly/daily mean-reversion edge. They show (a) event-dependent short-horizon dynamics, (b) a static calibration bias in political and long-dated markets, (c) fast quote-level reversion in the sum of legs, and (d) that naive market making loses to adverse selection.

1. Hourly/daily mean reversion: unproven. Only 2603.03152 looks at it (30 min / 3 h, 3 events): one reversal (about half retraced), one continuation, one muted. Gate any reversion strategy on shock type and linked-market confirmation; backtest it yourself on your own tick data.
2. Momentum: order-flow direction persists at 6 h (flow history ROC-AUC 0.78 in 2609.28965) and shocks with confirming linked moves continue (2603.03152). Use flow imbalance for inventory skew and quote withdrawal, not for outright bets.
3. Static bias is the cleaner edge: politics slope 1.2-1.8 (Kalshi), 1.2-1.5 (Polymarket); long-dated prices compressed toward 50%. Move the quoting reference price away from 50% for political/long-dated markets (with shrinkage and intercept); keep neutral for sports/crypto within 48 h. Unmeasured: net of fees and spread.
4. Fast reversion: the sum of complementary legs reverts in about 20 min half-life (Betfair, 2024). Quote around the fair sum; exploit with tight leg-pair repricing rather than directional views. Likely microstructure, not alpha net of costs.
5. Risk of reversals: markets near even odds with recent movement carry 5-7x reversal lift; widen/cut size there. Comment stance is not a usable signal alone.
6. Adverse selection dominates: after a shock, first 30 min flow is one-sided (94% at withdrawal) with high Kyle's lambda; pull quotes or widen. Track markout per fill and shut off any market/regime with negative 5-step markout.
7. Validation standard: out-of-sample by whole market/day, include fees and spread, report across seeds. None of the five papers reports net executable returns; treat their numbers as hypotheses to test on Polymarket/Kalshi data.

Open questions for backtests: (i) post-shock drift vs. shock type on many events, not three; (ii) calibration slope by domain x horizon net of spread on the actual quoted market; (iii) leg-sum deviation half-life on Polymarket CLOB (YES+NO) with fees; (iv) markout curve by time-of-day and flow-imbalance state.
